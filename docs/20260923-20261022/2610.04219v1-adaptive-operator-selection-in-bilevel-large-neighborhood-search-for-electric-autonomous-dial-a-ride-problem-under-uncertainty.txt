ABSTRACT 

The electric autonomous dial-a-ride problem (EADARP) extends the classical dial-a-ride problem by incorporating battery and charging constraints for electric vehicles. In practice, travel-time un-certainty can cause violations of time-window constraints. Large neighborhood search is effective for solving the EADARP, but its performance can depend on the choice of insertion operator during the repair phase. This paper investigates insertion-operator selection within a bilevel large neighbor-hood search framework for deterministic and chance-constrained variants of the EADARP. In the chance-constrained variant, arc travel times are modeled as independent normally distributed ran-dom variables, and upper time-window constraints are enforced probabilistically. We consider six selection methods, namely fixed greedy insertion, fixed regret-based insertion, random selection, a deterministic state-based rule, performance-adaptive ALNS selection, and LLM-based state-aware selection. Experimental results show comparable performance on smaller instances, while differ-ences become more evident on larger and more constrained instances. There is no single strategy that performs best across all instances, and the relative performance of the LLM-based, rule-based, and ALNS strategies varies with the problem instance and experimental setting.

1 Introduction 

The growing demand for urban transportation has increased interest in on-demand ride-sharing systems [1]. Com-pared with fixed-schedule public transportation, these services offer greater flexibility. They may also reduce oper-ating costs and environmental impacts relative to privately owned vehicles and conventional taxi services. Recent advances in electric and autonomous vehicle technologies enable energy-efficient and scalable operations. Electric vehicles introduce constraints related to battery capacity and charging, while autonomous vehicles enable continuous operation without human drivers and improve fleet utilization. The integration of these technologies leads to the elec-tric autonomous dial-a-ride problem (EADARP) [2], which extends the classical dial-a-ride problem (DARP) [3, 4] by incorporating battery and charging constraints. These additional constraints further increase the complexity of the already NP-hard DARP. Moreover, travel times in ride-sharing networks can vary due to traffic congestion, incidents, weather, and time-of-day effects. Ignoring such uncertainty may lead to time-window violations, particularly in tightly scheduled services with limited time buffer. To address this problem, chance constraints [5] can be incorporated into optimization problems such that stochastic constraints are satisfied with high probability, usually denoted by α. It has been successfully applied to optimization problems in domains such as mining [6, 7, 8] and logistics [9, 10, 11]. arXiv:2610.04219v1 [cs.NE] 3 Oct 2026 Adaptive Operator Selection in BI-LNS for Electric Autonomous Dial-a-Ride Problem under Uncertainty Large neighborhood search (LNS) is a well-established local search heuristic that uses a destroy operator to remove part of the current solution and a repair operator to reconstruct the resulting partial solution at each iteration [12]. In the literature, a variety of operators have been proposed for request removal and insertion. Common removal operators include random and worst removal, while popular insertion operators include greedy, deep-greedy, and regret-based insertion [13, 14]. LNS is an established method to solve DARP [15, 16, 17], EADARP [18, 19, 20, 21], and other routing, scheduling, and location problems [22, 23]. Furthermore, recent advances in artificial intelligence and natural language processing have led to the rapid devel-opment of large language models (LLMs). These models have been successfully applied across a wide range of applications, including decision support systems [24, 25, 26] and tools for algorithm design [27, 28, 29]. In this paper, we investigate insertion-operator selection within a bilevel large neighborhood search framework for deterministic and chance-constrained EADARP variants. We compare fixed greedy, fixed regret, random, deterministic rule-based, performance-adaptive ALNS, and LLM-based state-aware selection while keeping all other components of the BI-LNS framework unchanged. 

1.1 Related Work 

Several exact and metaheuristic approaches have been proposed for solving the EADARP. Bongiovanni et al. [30] introduced the problem and proposed three- and two-index MILP formulations, demonstrating improved scalability of the two-index model. Subsequently, Bongiovanni et al. [31] investigated the dynamic EADARP using a greedy construction combined with a machine learning–assisted large neighborhood search. Due to the limited scalability of exact methods, several metaheuristic approaches have been developed, including deterministic annealing with battery-restricted fragments [32] and the bilevel large neighborhood search (BI-LNS) framework [18]. In BI-LNS, an outer level optimizes charging schedules, while an inner level optimizes pickup and delivery requests. More recent studies further extended LNS-based approaches with dynamic charging-station insertion strategies [19, 20, 21, 33, 34]. More-over, early work by Ropke and Pisinger [13] examined operator selection in LNS, proposing adaptive mechanisms that choose among multiple heuristics using historical performance information. Chance-constrained optimization [5] provides a promising framework for handling uncertainty by ensuring that con-straints are satisfied with a specified probability [35, 36, 37]. In the mining domain, Stimson et al. [8] applied a single-objective evolutionary algorithm to chance-constrained open-pit mine scheduling. More recently, a bi-objective evolutionary formulation was developed for dynamic chance-constrained open-pit mine scheduling, where block eco-nomic values are stochastic and mining and processing capacities vary over time [38]. In the context of DARP, Johnsen et al. [11] investigated a stochastic variant with interrelated trips. They proposed a chance-constrained formulation that approximates arrival- and service-time distributions and evaluates probabilistic feasibility within an adaptive variable neighborhood search framework. Recent studies have also explored LLMs as tools for decision support and automated algorithm design in optimization. Zhong et al. [26] integrated LLMs into hyper-heuristic evolutionary frameworks, where the LLM acts as a high-level controller for selecting or sequencing low-level heuristics. Liu et al. [27] provided a systematic review outlining the roles and limitations of LLMs across the algorithm design pipeline. In addition, language-driven hyper-heuristics have been proposed for NP-hard combinatorial optimization, enabling automated heuristic generation and adaptation with minimal manual intervention [29]. Despite these advances, most EADARP studies consider deterministic travel times, and the effect of insertion-operator selection within BI-LNS remains underexplored. In particular, fixed, random, rule-based, and adaptive selection strategies have not been compared for deterministic and chance-constrained EADARP variants. 

1.2 Contribution 

This paper investigates inner-level insertion-operator selection within BI-LNS for classical and chance-constrained variants of the EADARP. Building on the BI-LNS framework introduced in [18], we study how different selection strategies affect feasibility and solution quality while keeping the remaining components of the algorithm unchanged. We compare six insertion-operator selection strategies, including greedy insertion, regret-based insertion, random selection, deterministic rule-based selection, adaptive ALNS selection, and LLM-based state-aware selection. We introduce BI-LNS-LLM, in which an LLM selects between greedy and regret-based insertion using compact features that describe the current search state. The LLM does not construct routes, check feasibility, or evaluate objective values. 2Adaptive Operator Selection in BI-LNS for Electric Autonomous Dial-a-Ride Problem under Uncertainty We also consider a chance-constrained EADARP in which arc travel times are modeled as independent, normally distributed random variables, while all other problem parameters remain deterministic. Upper time-window constraints are enforced probabilistically using chance constraints. The remainder of this paper presents the deterministic and chance-constrained EADARP formulations in Sections 2 and 3, respectively. Section 4 describes the BI-LNS framework and operator-selection strategies. Sections 5 and 6 present the experimental setup and results, respectively, and Section 7 concludes the paper. 

2 Problem Definition 

This section defines the electric autonomous dial-a-ride problem, including its objective function and constraints. 

2.1 Problem Setting 

The EADARP extends the classical DARP by incorporating battery and charging constraints for electric autonomous vehicles (EAVs). The problem considered in this work is based on the deterministic EADARP variant of Limmer [18], itself derived from the formulation introduced by Bongiovanni et al. [30]. We define the EADARP as a complete directed graph G = ( V, A ), where V denotes the set of locations and A = {(i, j ) | i, j ∈ V, i ̸ = j} denotes the set of arcs. A fleet of K electric autonomous vehicles is available to serve N transportation requests over a planning horizon of length T .Let K = {1, . . . , K } denote the set of vehicles. The vertex set V is partitioned into five disjoint subsets: the set of pickup locations P = {1, . . . , N }, the corresponding set of drop-off locations D = {N + 1 , . . . , 2N }, the set of start depots O, the set of end depots F , and the set of charging stations S. Each transportation request i ∈ P consists of a pickup at location i and a corresponding drop-off at location i + N ∈ D. Each vehicle k ∈ K starts its route at a designated start depot ok ∈ O and must terminate at an end depot fk ∈ F .

4 Insertion-Operator Selection in Bilevel Large Neighborhood Search 

This section presents the baseline BI-LNS framework and the six insertion-operator selection strategies. 

4.1 BI-LNS framework 

To solve both the deterministic and chance-constrained variants of the EADARP, we adapt the bilevel large neighbor-hood search framework introduced by Limmer [18]. BI-LNS separates charging and request-routing decisions. The outer level inserts, removes, or modifies charging sessions, while the inner level arranges pickup and drop-off locations around the fixed charging sessions. Each charging session specifies a station, a charging duration, and an upper bound on its completion time. The algorithm starts with one empty route for each vehicle and generates Cinit charging sessions for each route. It then calls the inner search to insert the passenger requests. At each outer iteration, it selects charging session modification, insertion, or removal with probabilities pmod , pins , and prem , respectively. If the solution contains no charging sessions, it selects insertion. After changing a route, the algorithm removes all passenger requests from that route and calls the inner search to reinsert them. We consider unlimited charging-station visits where a station can appear in multiple charging sessions. The outer search prioritizes the number of served requests until it finds a complete solution. 

4.2 Inner-level search 

For fixed charging sessions, the inner level applies a destroy-and-repair search. It first orders the requests by increasing lower bound of their pickup time windows and attempts to insert them. Requests that cannot be inserted remain rejected. Each inner iteration starts from the best retained solution. The destroy step randomly removes qout served requests. The algorithm combines these requests with the rejected set and selects up to qin requests for reinsertion. It orders the selected requests by their pickup time-window lower bounds and applies the insertion operator chosen by the corresponding selection strategy. Before a complete solution is found, the algorithm starts with Qin and Qout and adjusts these values according to the number of rejected requests. After all requests are served, it sets qin = qout = Q. We use the same adjustment in all six variants. The inner search follows a feasibility-first acceptance rule. While the best solution is incomplete, it accepts a route-feasible candidate if the candidate does not increase the number of rejected requests. It accepts a route-infeasible candidate only when the candidate strictly reduces that number. After finding a complete solution, it accepts only complete candidates that do not increase the objective value. Algorithm 1 summarizes the inner search. For the deterministic EADARP, the algorithm applies the feasibility conditions in Section 2. For the chance-constrained variant, it uses the time-window evaluation in Section 3. Therefore, the selection strategy changes only the insertion operator, not the constraints or their evaluation. 

4.3 Insertion operators 

We use greedy and regret-based insertion because they provide different repair behaviors. Greedy insertion focuses on the best immediate move, while regret insertion prioritizes requests that may become difficult to insert later.

5 Experimental Setting 

In this section, we present the experimental setup used to evaluate our approach on deterministic and chance-constrained EADARP instances. We use two benchmark sets derived from classical DARP instances. The first set contains ten instances introduced in [42], and the second contains ten instances introduced in [43]. Following [30], we extend these instances by adding battery constraints and three charging stations. We denote an instance by aK -N , where K is the number of vehicles and N is the number of transportation requests. For the deterministic experiments, consistent with prior EADARP studies [18, 19], we set the minimum final battery ratio to γ = 0.7. We also examine the effect of this parameter on the Ropke instances using γ ∈{0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9}. Following previous EADARP studies [18, 19], we set the objective weights in Equa-tion (1) to w1 = 0 .75 and w2 = 0 .25 .For the chance-constrained experiments, we use coefficients of variation CV ∈ { 0.1, 0.3} and confidence levels

We set the initial number of charging sessions per vehicle to Cinit = 1 . At the outer level, we select charging-session modification, insertion, and removal with probabilities 0.55 , 0.225 , and 0.225 , respectively. 6.1.1 Impact of Minimum Final Battery Ratio γ

We analyze the effect of the minimum final battery ratio γ on the Ropke instances. Figure 2 shows the conditional mean objective value for each BI-LNS variant and battery level. Lower values are better, and blank cells indicate that the corresponding method obtained no feasible run. For γ ≤ 0.5, most methods obtain similar objective values within each instance. This indicates that the insertion-operator selection strategy has a limited effect when the battery requirement is relatively weak. From γ = 0 .7,objective values generally increase because solutions must preserve a higher battery level at the end of each route. Meeting this requirement restricts request allocation, route construction, and charging decisions. The effect of increasing γ depends strongly on the instance. All six methods obtain feasible solutions at γ = 0 .9

on a6-48, a6-60, a7-70, and a8-64. In contrast, only BI-LNS-Random obtains a feasible run at γ = 0 .9 on a5-60 and a6-72, while only BI-LNS-Greedy and BI-LNS-Random do so on a7-56. On a7-84, only BI-LNS-Regret and BI-LNS-Rule obtain feasible runs at this battery level. No method obtains a feasible run at γ = 0 .9 on a8-80 or a8-96. At γ = 0 .8 on a8-96, only BI-LNS-Random obtains a feasible run. These results show that stricter battery requirements increase both objective values and the difficulty of feasibility recovery. However, the method that remains feasible under the strictest setting varies across instances. Therefore, there is no single selection strategy that performs best across all instances and battery settings. We provide detailed objective values and feasibility rates in the Appendix A.

7 Conclusions 

In this paper, we investigated the deterministic and chance-constrained variants of the electric autonomous dial-a-ride problem. In the chance-constrained setting, we modeled travel times as independent normal random variables and enforced upper time-window constraints using deterministic approximations of chance constraints. We compared fixed greedy, fixed regret-k, random, rule-based, performance-adaptive, and LLM-based inner level insertion operator selection within a common BI-LNS framework. In the deterministic problem, the computational results show that operator selection has little effect on small instances, where all methods recover feasibility. Its effect is larger on more constrained instances. No strategy consistently achieves both the highest feasibility rate and the lowest conditional mean objective. BI-LNS-LLM obtains the low-est conditional mean on several instances, while the other strategies perform best on others and sometimes recover feasibility more frequently. In the chance-constrained experiments, feasibility generally decreases as the final battery requirement, confidence level, and travel-time variability increase. BI-LNS-ALNS and BI-LNS-LLM obtain competitive conditional objective values in several configurations, but neither method consistently dominates in solution quality or feasibility. Overall, LLM-based selection provides a state-aware alternative for choosing insertion operators, but it does not consistently outperform simpler selection strategies. Future work will explore enhanced state representations, alternative prompting strategies, and extensions to other stochastic and dynamic vehicle routing problems.
