## Abstract 

The Distributed Flexible Job Shop Scheduling Problem (DFJSP) requires the joint determination of factory assignment, machine se-lection, and operation sequencing to minimize the makespan. This paper presents an exact SAT-based approach built on an efficient SAT formulation that exploits problem structure through propaga-tion, symmetry breaking, and incremental SAT solving to effectively address these coupled decisions. The proposed method is evaluated on 76 standard benchmark instances from the Fattahi, Brandimarte, and rdata datasets under two- and three-factory configurations. To-gether with previously established results, the new SAT optimality certificates bring the total number of proven-optimal instances to 72 out of 76, including three newly certified instances; for two of these instances, SAT also improves the previous best-known makespans. Across the benchmark configurations, SAT achieves the best results on four of the five configurations and remains competitive with strong exact approaches such as CP and LBBD1_CPIL, as well as existing metaheuristic methods. These results demonstrate that the proposed SAT framework is an effective exact approach for the DFJSP, combining strong solution quality with rigorous optimality certification and offering a complementary exact method for future DFJSP studies and evaluations on additional benchmark instances.

## 1 Introduction 

Production scheduling determines how operations are assigned to manufacturing resources and ordered over time while satisfying technological and capacity constraints. As manufacturing systems become more flexible, these decisions become more difficult because an operation may be processed by different eligible resources rather than by a single predefined machine [7].

The Job Shop Scheduling Problem (JSSP) [10] represents the clas-sical setting in which each operation must be processed on a prede-fined machine. The Flexible Job Shop Scheduling Problem (FJSP) [4] extends this setting by allowing each operation to be processed on one of several eligible machines. Solving the FJSP therefore requires machine selection to be considered together with opera-tion sequencing, which increases the difficulty of the scheduling problem [3, 9, 12, 17]. The Distributed Flexible Job Shop Schedul-ing Problem (DFJSP), introduced by De Giovanni and Pezzella [8], further extends the FJSP to a multi-factory setting. In the DFJSP, each job is assigned to a single factory, and all of its operations are processed within that factory. A feasible schedule must therefore determine the factory assigned to each job, select an eligible ma-chine for each operation, and determine the processing order of operations competing for the same machine [8, 18]. A wide range of heuristic and metaheuristic methods has been proposed for the DFJSP, including genetic algorithms, chemical reaction optimization, and hybrid metaheuristics [5, 8, 15, 16, 19]. These methods can produce high-quality schedules for relatively large instances and have improved existing benchmark solutions. However, they do not in general provide certificates of optimality or rigorous lower bounds. Consequently, when a good feasible solution is obtained, it may remain unknown whether a better schedule exists. Exact methods address this limitation by providing optimality guarantees. Meng et al. [18] developed several Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) formulations and a Constraint Programming (CP) model for the DFJSP, while Xiong and Liu [20] later proposed Logic-based Benders Decomposition approaches that separate factory assignment from scheduling decisions. These methods have substantially advanced the exact solution of DFJSP benchmark instances. Nevertheless, proving optimality remains difficult for some medium- and large-scale cases within practical computational limits [18, 20]. These observations motivate the investigation of alternative ex-act approaches with two complementary capabilities. First, an exact method should be able to determine whether a known solution is optimal and, when possible, provide a proof of optimality. Second, it should remain effective in searching for improved feasible solutions when the current best-known makespan has not yet been certified. A method combining these capabilities is particularly valuable for benchmark-driven research because it can both enlarge the set of in-stances with proven optimal solutions and improve the best-known results for unresolved cases. Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) provides an alternative exact frame-work for discrete optimization. Learning SAT solvers combine Boolean propagation, clause learn-ing, conflict analysis, and adaptive search strategies to efficiently solve large combinatorial problems [2]. SAT-based formulations have also been successfully investigated for scheduling and related optimization problems [6, 11, 14]. In particular, previous studies on the classical JSSP have shown that SAT can effectively repre-sent temporal and machine-conflict decisions while supporting makespan minimization through repeated feasibility tests [11, 14]. The DFJSP, however, introduces additional decisions that are ab-sent from the classical JSSP, most notably job-to-factory assignment and alternative machine selection. These decisions interact with operation timing and resource conflicts and introduce additional symmetry when factories are identical. To the best of our knowl-edge, a dedicated SAT-based exact formulation for the DFJSP has not previously been studied. This work addresses this gap by de-veloping a SAT-based approach for the minimum-makespan DFJSP and evaluating its ability both to certify optimality and to improve previously reported benchmark solutions. The main contributions of this work are as follows: 

• We propose an exact SAT-based approach for the DFJSP that exploits problem structure through an efficient formulation, problem-specific propagation, symmetry breaking, and in-cremental SAT solving. 

• We provide a systematic computational evaluation on 76 stan-dard benchmark instances from the Fattahi, Brandimarte [3], and rdata [12] datasets. The results establish three new opti-mality certificates, two of which correspond to instances for which SAT improves the previous best-known makespans, and, together with previously established results, show that 72 of the 76 instances have now been proven optimal. 

• We provide an additional exact approach for the DFJSP that offers competitive solution quality and rigorous optimality certification, serving as a reference for future studies and evaluations on additional benchmark instances. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 defines the DFJSP and reviews related work. Section 3 presents the proposed SAT formulation and the incremental optimization proce-dure. Section 4 describes the benchmark instances, experimental setup, and computational results. Finally, Section 5 concludes the paper and outlines directions for future research.

## 2.2 Related Work 

Research on the DFJSP has mainly focused on two broad classes of solution approaches: heuristic and metaheuristic methods for efficiently obtaining feasible schedules, and exact methods aimed at providing optimality guarantees. More recently, advances in exact optimization have also led to decomposition-based approaches that exploit the hierarchical structure of factory assignment and shop-level scheduling. Heuristic and metaheuristic approaches have been extensively investigated for the DFJSP because of the large search space aris-ing from factory assignment, machine selection, and operation sequencing. Representative approaches include genetic algorithms, chemical reaction optimization, and other problem-specific search procedures [5, 8, 15, 16]. More recently, Xie et al. [19] proposed a Hybrid Genetic Tabu Search Algorithm (HGTSA) that combines genetic operators with critical-path-based tabu search and reported 13 new upper bounds on 69 benchmark instances. These approaches have played an important role in the search for competitive feasi-ble schedules and in improving known benchmark upper bounds. However, heuristic and metaheuristic methods do not generally provide valid lower bounds or certificates of optimality. Exact approaches complement heuristic search by providing mechanisms for establishing optimality. Meng et al. [18] developed four Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) formulations and a Constraint Programming (CP) model for the DFJSP. Their compu-tational results showed that the CP model scales more effectively than the MILP formulations on larger instances. The study estab-lished optimal solutions for 62 benchmark instances and reported 11 new best solutions, showing that exact methods can contribute both to optimality certification and to the improvement of known upper bounds. More recently, Xiong and Liu [20] proposed Logic-based Benders Decomposition (LBBD) approaches that separate job-to-factory assignment from the scheduling subproblems as-sociated with individual factories. Their best-performing variant, LBBD1_CPIL, combines a master assignment problem with CP-based scheduling and reported an average optimality gap below 1.2% over 286 benchmark instances. These results substantially ex-tended the range of DFJSP instances that can be addressed within an exact optimization framework, although proving optimality re-mains difficult for some medium- and large-scale instances. Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) provides another exact paradigm for combinatorial scheduling and has previously been investigated for the classical Job Shop Scheduling Problem. Koshimura et al. [14] formulated makespan minimization as a sequence of SAT fea-sibility problems using Boolean variables to represent temporal and machine-ordering decisions. Their approach proved optimal makespans for previously unresolved benchmark instances and im-proved several known bounds. Huang and Zhou [11] subsequently developed a more compact SAT formulation by restricting tempo-ral variables and clauses to feasible time ranges and introducing precedence variables only for operations that can compete for the same machine. These studies indicate that SAT can be effective for exact scheduling when the Boolean formulation exploits problem-specific structure to strengthen propagation and avoid unnecessary variables and clauses. However, SAT formulations developed for the classical JSSP do not directly capture the additional decisions introduced by the DFJSP. In the JSSP, each operation is assigned to a predefined ma-chine and all operations are processed within a single shop. In the DFJSP, each job must additionally be assigned to a factory, while each operation must select one of several eligible machines. Conse-quently, machine-capacity conflicts are conditional on both factory and machine assignments: two operations compete for the same physical resource only when their jobs are assigned to the same fac-tory and both operations select the same machine. Moreover, identi-cal factories introduce symmetries associated with interchangeable factory labels. These characteristics require factory assignment, machine selection, temporal decisions, and conditional resource conflicts to be represented jointly within the Boolean formulation. Existing exact approaches for the DFJSP have primarily relied on MILP, CP, and decomposition-based methods, while SAT-based scheduling studies have addressed various scheduling settings. The proposed SAT formulation jointly models factory assignment, machine selection, timing, precedence, and conditional machine-capacity constraints, supported by temporal preprocessing, propa-gation, symmetry breaking, and incremental SAT solving.

## 4 Computational Experiments 4.1 Benchmark Instances 

We evaluate the proposed SAT method on three widely used FJSP benchmark suites: Brandimarte [3], Fattahi et al. [9], and the rdata 

subset of Hurink et al. [12]. Since these benchmarks were originally designed for single-factory production, we use the distributed ver-sions adopted in previous DFJSP studies [18, 19], in which identical factories share the same machine configurations and processing capabilities. The Fattahi set contains 10 instances and is evalu-ated with two factories, while the Brandimarte set contains 10 instances and is tested with both two and three factories. The rdata Research Paper Track AAMAS 2027, 3–7 May 2027, Hanoi, Vietnam 

set contains 23 instances and is also evaluated with two and three factories. We omit the four-factory rdata setting because previous studies [18, 19] show that almost all instances already attain their theoretical lower bounds with three factories, while the four-factory configuration becomes nearly trivial and therefore provides limited discriminatory value. 

## 4.2 Experimental Setup and Metrics 

All SAT experiments are conducted on a Google Cloud Compute En-gine c4-standard-4 virtual machine. The proposed encoding is imple-mented in Python using PySAT [13], with CaDiCaL version 1.9.5 [1] as the underlying solver. Each instance is evaluated under 600 s and 1800 s time limits, including preprocessing, encoding construction, and solving. The 1800 s run is omitted when the corresponding instance has already been proven optimal within 600 s. The proposed method is compared with the MILP and CP models of Meng et al. [18], HGTSA [19], and LBBD1_CPIL [20]. For the CP model of Meng et al., two sets of results are reported separately, namely the original 600 s results from Meng et al. [18] and the 1800 s results obtained by Xiong et al. [20] using the same model. All baseline results are taken from the corresponding publications or publicly released experimental data, whereas only the proposed SAT method is executed on the platform described above. HGTSA [19] is run for up to 4000 iterations rather than un-der a fixed time limit. As a metaheuristic, it does not itself prove optimality; therefore, a reported HGTSA solution is regarded as optimal when its makespan matches the corresponding reported lower bound. To ensure consistency with previous literature, we consider only the 20 two-factory rdata instances reported by Xiong and Liu [20] (for LBBD1_CPIL and CP) that match the benchmark set used in earlier studies. ## 4.3 Results and Analysis 

Table 3 summarizes the computational results across the five bench-mark configurations. Within 600 s, SAT obtains the best makespan among the compared methods on 68 of the 76 instances and proves optimality for 66 instances. Extending the unresolved runs to 1800 s increases these numbers to 69 and 67, respectively. SAT proves all Fattahi instances optimal, matching the complete result previously reported for MILP, and also solves all three-factory Brandimarte and 

rdata instances to proven optimality within 600 s. The remaining unresolved cases are concentrated in the two-factory Brandimarte and rdata configurations, where the smaller number of factories leads to stronger competition for machine resources and more re-strictive scheduling decisions. Across the five benchmark configura-tions, SAT achieves or matches the strongest overall performance in four of them: two-factory Brandimarte, three-factory Brandimarte, two-factory Fattahi, and three-factory rdata . The exception is the two-factory rdata configuration, where HGTSA obtains the best makespan on all 23 instances, although SAT still proves 17 instances optimal and remains competitive in solution quality. The two-factory configurations provide the most informative comparison among the exact approaches. On Brandimarte, SAT and the CP rerun of Xiong et al. [18] and SAT each prove 17 of the 23 in-stances optimal. LBBD1_CPIL proves 15 of the 20 reported instances optimal and obtains the best makespan on 16 of them. These results indicate that SAT is competitive not only in op-timality certification but also in solution quality on the unresolved instances. The difference between the two- and three-factory configura-tions is noticeable for Brandimarte and rdata . With three factories, SAT proves all 10 Brandimarte and all 23 rdata instances optimal within 600 s. For Brandimarte, the CP rerun reaches the same best makespan on all 10 instances but certifies only eight within 1800 s, while LBBD1_CPIL certifies five. For rdata , the previously reported results for CP and HGTSA attain the corresponding best values on 22 of the 23 instances, whereas SAT proves all 23 optimal. These results suggest that increasing the number of factories substantially reduces the difficulty associated with machine-resource conflicts, despite enlarging the factory-assignment decision space. ## 5 Conclusion 

This paper has presented an exact SAT-based approach for the Distributed Flexible Job Shop Scheduling Problem (DFJSP) as a complementary alternative to existing exact methods. The pro-posed formulation jointly captures factory assignment, machine selection, operation timing, precedence relations, and conditional machine-capacity conflicts, while exploiting problem structure through temporal-domain reduction, propagation, symmetry break-ing, and incremental SAT solving. The resulting framework enables both effective search for feasible schedules and rigorous certifica-tion of optimality, as demonstrated by the computational results on standard DFJSP benchmarks. Computational results demonstrate the effectiveness of the pro-posed approach across 76 standard benchmark instances. Together with previously established results, the new SAT optimality cer-tificates bring the total number of proven-optimal instances to 72 out of 76, including three newly certified instances. Moreover, SAT improves the previous best-known makespans for two of these instances: MK10 with two factories, from 130 to 125, and la15 with three factories, from 388 to 387, with both improved makespans proven optimal. For MK08 with three factories, SAT also proves the incumbent best-known makespan of 184 to be optimal. Across the benchmark configurations, SAT achieves the best results on four of the five configurations and remains competitive with strong exact approaches, including CP and LBBD1_CPIL, as well as existing metaheuristic methods. Future work will investigate stronger propagation mechanisms and more compact resource-conflict encodings for larger instances. Integrating SAT with heuristic warm-start strategies and tighter lower bounds is another promising direction. The framework can also be extended to more complex DFJSP variants, including se-quencing flexibility, heterogeneous factories, and transportation times, and evaluated on additional benchmark instances.
