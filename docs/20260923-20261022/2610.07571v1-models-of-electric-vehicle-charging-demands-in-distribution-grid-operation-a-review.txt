# Models of Electric Vehicle Charging Demands in Distribution Grid Operation: A Review 

Dipayan Sarkar 

Electrical and Computer Engineering University of Central Florida Orlando, USA dipayan.sarkar@ucf.edu 

Qifeng Li 

Electrical and Computer Engineering University of Central Florida Orlando, USA Qifeng.Li@ucf.edu 

Abstract —The rapid integration of electric vehicles (EVs) into the transportation system has introduced a new class of load into power distribution systems (PDS), i.e., EV charging demand (EVCD). The spatiotemporal variability of EVCD poses fundamental challenges for distribution system operators (DSOs), particularly in ensuring secure and reliable network operation. Accurately representing EVCD as a parameter within the distri-bution optimal power flow (DOPF) problem is a prerequisite for reliable, cost-efficient PDS operation, yet no universally accepted modeling framework has emerged. The existing con-tributions span a wide spectrum of assumptions, mathematical formulations, and solution paradigms. This article presents a systematic review of methods and models employed to represent EVCD within DOPF frameworks from the DSO’s operational perspective. The existing EVCD modeling methods are classified into two primary categories based on EVCD representation: deterministic models and uncertainty-aware models. The two main categories are further divided into several subcategories. This paper aims to provide a comprehensive review on models for integrating EVCD into PDS operation and planning. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

With the global transition toward clean energy, electric vehicle (EV) adoption has accelerated significantly in recent years. Global EV charging demand (EVCD) reached 250 TWh in 2025 and is projected to grow to 800 TWh by 2030, potentially exceeding 1500 TWh by 2035 [1], as illustrated in Fig. 1. This trajectory implies that EVCD, which accounted for approximately 1% of global electricity consumption in 2025, will increase nearly sixfold within a decade and will place a substantial stress on power distribution system (PDS). EVCD can be modeled based on driver mobility patterns [2], arrival time (AT) and departure time (DT) [3], initial state-of-charge (SOC) of the battery [4], and the required energy [5]. Researchers proposed different methods to model EVCD in some of the existing literature that are either from EV aggregator (EVA) perspective [6] or for EV charging station (EVCS) planning [7] without showing how to integrate them into the PDS operation [8]. However, if EVCD is not properly modeled and incorporated into the PDS operation, PDS will experience voltage violations, thermal overloading of lines and transformers, increased power losses, and degraded power quality [9], [10]. To ensure that this review reflects recent developments in EV charging demand modeling, the literature was collected from Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore, and Google Scholar using combinations of terms including EV charging demand, uncertain EV demand, machine learning-based EV charging, EV demand uncertainty, EV demand in distribution systems, and EV charging loads. The search was conducted across titles, abstracts, and keywords of the databases, and only publications from 2018 onward were included. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Sec-tion II presents an overview of the reviewed EVCD models and the motivation behind their classification. Section III details the EVCD models across all categories. Section IV discusses research gaps and future directions before making concluding remarks in Section V. II. EVCD M ODELING : O VERVIEW AND CLASSIFICATION 

The accurate representation of EVCD is essential for any credible DOPF formulation from the distribution system operator (DSO) perspective [33]. However, no generalized modeling framework currently exists that can capture EVCD with complete accuracy and certainty. As a result, EVCD modeling within DOPF spans a broad spectrum, ranging from deterministic representations [34] to sophisticated uncertainty-aware formulations that capture the stochastic and decision-dependent nature of EV charging behavior [32]. To improve prediction accuracy, machine learning (ML)-based approaches have been proposed, where historical data are utilized to forecast future charging demand [36], [37]. In addition, several deterministic formulations incorporate spe-cific EV characteristics, enabling EVCD to be modeled either by the EVA or the DSO, either directly [38] or through coordination with the transportation system operator (TSO) [39]. The resulting EVCD is subsequently treated as a known input parameter in the DOPF problem. However, in practical systems, complete information about EV-related parameters is not available to any system operator. Uncertainty arises from multiple sources, including, but not limited to AT, DT and SOC. Such uncertainties may origi-nate from external sources, i.e., exogenous uncertainty (ExU) [11]–[13], [15] or be induced by system-level decisions, i.e., endogenous uncertainty (EnU) [17], [29], [31], [32]. These uncertainties in EVCD are incorporated into DOPF formula-tions and addressed using established optimization paradigms, including stochastic optimization (SO), robust optimization (RO), chance-constrained optimization (CCO), distributionally robust optimization (DRO), and distributionally robust chance-constrained optimization (DRCCO). 2) ML-Based Predictive Models: ML-based predictive models forecast future EVCD from historical load data, and this EVCD is used as a known parameter in the DOPF. ML model forecasting is a two-phase procedure: a training phase in which model parameters are estimated, followed by an inference phase in which demand forecasts are gen-erated over the dispatch horizon. Supervised methods such as extreme gradient boosting, linear regression [53], support vector machine, and random forest [54] have been widely employed for short-term forecasting, while unsupervised ap-proaches including autoregressive integrated moving average [55] and K-means clustering [56] have been applied to demand characterization and load profile extraction. Deep learning architectures including long short-term memory networks [57], [58], deep generative models [59], artificial neural networks [60], convolutional neural networks [61], transformer models [62], ensemble methods [63], and transfer learning approaches [64], have been proposed for spatiotemporal EVCD forecast-ing, owing to their capacity to capture nonlinear temporal dependencies. 

B. Uncertainty-aware Models 

In practical systems, EVCD is inherently uncertain due to variability in factors such as electricity price, SOC, AT, DT, EV owner preferences and decisions. To capture these uncer-tainties, a range of modeling approaches has been proposed in the literature. In this review, uncertainty-aware EVCD models are classified into four categories based on the underlying solution methodology. 

1) SO Model: In SO-based EVCD models, key EV param-eters are treated as random variables characterized by known probability distributions. Parameters commonly modeled as stochastic include AT, DT, required charging energy, and initial SOC, which are represented using distributions such as Poisson [11], [65], uniform [11], normal [66], lognormal [18], or truncated normal [12], [26].

The reviewed literature reveals several persistent gaps in EVCD-DOPF frameworks. A comparison of EVCD modeling approaches is given in Table II. The overwhelming majority of uncertainty-aware formulations treat EVCD as an exogenous uncertain parameter independent of DSO decisions [11], [12], [15], [25], despite the fact that DSO pricing and dispatch sig-nals directly influence EV owner charging behavior. Although recent contributions have begun addressing this by modeling EVCD as endogenous uncertainty shaped by real-time DSO decisions [17], [29], [31], [32], such formulations remain scarce. Furthermore, existing models predominantly assume a homogeneous EV owner population; while isolated efforts have incorporated owner behavior through survey data [68] or willingness-to-charge formulations [69], explicit integration of behavioral heterogeneity into a coupled EVCD-DOPF frame-work remains largely absent. A further structural gap exists in the treatment of transportation-power system coupling: studies accounting for TS-PDS interactions have adopted deterministic EVCD representations [52], while uncertainty-aware formula-tions have largely omitted TS dynamics [14], [17]. Finally, the diversity of approaches reviewed reflects the absence of a generalized EVCD formulation encompassing all rele-vant EV parameters and system interactions. Future research should therefore prioritize the development of uncertainty-aware EVCD models that incorporate behavioral heterogeneity and explicitly capture TS-PDS coupling within a unified DOPF framework. V. C ONCLUSION 

This paper has presented a systematic review of EVCD modeling and its integration into the DOPF framework, ex-amined exclusively from the DSO’s perspective. The reviewed literature was classified according to the nature of EVCD representation and the solution methodology employed. Ac-curate EVCD modeling remains a fundamentally challenging problem, as charging demand is governed by multiple inter-dependent parameters across different systems and cannot be reduced to a simple deterministic expression. Moreover, no consensus has emerged on whether EVCD should be treated as a deterministic parameter, an exogenous uncertainty, or an endogenous uncertainty, and no existing model compre-hensively accounts for all relevant parameters and system interactions. By systematically surveying and categorizing the existing methods and their solution approaches from the DSO’s perspective, this review provides a structured reference for researchers, and establishes a foundation for the develop-ment of a generalized, unified EVCD modeling framework.
