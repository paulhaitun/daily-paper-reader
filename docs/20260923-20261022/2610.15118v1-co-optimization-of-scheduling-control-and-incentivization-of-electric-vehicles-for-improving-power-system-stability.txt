# Co-Optimization of Scheduling, Control, and Incentivization of Electric Vehicles for Improving Power System Stability 

Amit Kumer Podder, Tomonori Sadamoto, and Aranya Chakrabortty 

Abstract —We present a finite-horizon, greedy heuristic-based co-optimization and optimal control framework that jointly de-termines the optimized charging rates, charging times, and an op-timal charging control law for electric vehicles (EVs) to improve both voltage stability and small-signal dynamic performance of a power distribution system. The proposed strategy encourages EV drivers to be flexible in choosing their charging rates and times through monetary incentivization under a fixed budget. The design is validated using the IEEE 33-bus distribution system model with ten EV charging stations (EVCSs). Results show that incentivizing drivers to settle for lower charging rates, implemented by a wait-and-save strategy, and rescheduling their charging times strategically can significantly improve the voltage stability index (VSI) of the system as well as the damping factors for the mid-frequency oscillation modes seen in the DC-link bus voltage. 

Index Terms —Electric vehicles, optimal charging, temporal scheduling, incentivization, co-optimization, voltage stability

# INcreased adoption of electric vehicles (EVs), especially heavy-duty EVs such as electric trucks and electric buses, has led to the emergence of diverse charging solutions, in-cluding medium-voltage AC chargers in parking lots and DC fast chargers at public charging stations [1]. As the number of EVs scales and becomes an integral part of highway traffic, one challenge that distribution system operators are unanimously concerned about is on how to schedule, control, and coordinate the charging of several hundred EVs within a short period of time to avoid unexpected peak load pressure that can easily cause fluctuations in voltage and frequency at critical buses in the system. Multiple papers have been written on optimal EV charging and scheduling, such as [2]– [6], but mostly aimed at steady-state operations such as load flattening, voltage and frequency regulation, electricity pricing, and modeling of the equilibrium behavior of EV queues. Fair allocation of benefits between EV charging station (EVCS) owners and EV customers has been addressed in [7] using game theory, and monetary incentivization of EV customers to encourage flexible charging has been studied in [8], but, again, from a steady-state or quasi-steady-state point of view. Therefore, most of the existing optimal charging scheduling solutions are carried out in AC distribution networks by solving static convex or non-convex optimization problems without accounting for the dynamics coming from the power-electronics-based EVCS side. Moreover, these methods pri-marily target slow and medium-speed chargers by considering them as aggregated passive loads, making them unsuitable for emerging AC/DC distribution networks where DC fast charging stations, interfaced via voltage source converters and governed by fast-acting local control logic, are integrated into the power distribution grid. This raises a critical question: how can we design coordinated charging strategies that not only account for the dynamic behavior of high-power DC fast charging stations but also ensure better steady-state voltage stability index (VSI) within the distribution grid? To address this gap, in our recent paper [9], we proposed a co-design method where a system operator can dictate the 

optimal charging rate for an EV (that may be less than the demanded rate) and design an optimal charging control law using state-feedback linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) con-trol such that the combined effect of the two significantly improves the small-signal dynamic performance and VSI of the distribution network. The improvement came at the cost of incentivizing EV drivers to wait an additional amount of time in the charging queue, referred to as wait-and-save . The strategy was shown to be highly effective in damping the fast and medium-frequency oscillation modes seen in the temporal responses of the DC-link voltages at the charging stations following sudden plug-ins and plug-outs of the EV loads. The co-design was developed, however, assuming an infinite time horizon for the LQR, meaning that vehicles were assumed to arrive at EVCSs randomly over time without following any predetermined schedule. In this paper, we study how the additional flexibility of 

scheduling EVs for charging at different charging stations at different times of the day can help in improving grid dynamics even further. We present a finite-horizon, greedy heuristic-based co-optimization and optimal control framework that jointly determines the charging rates, charging times, and a LQR control law to improve voltage stability and small-signal dynamic performance by encouraging EV drivers to be flexible in choosing their charging rates and times in exchange for monetary incentives. Unlike [9], however, the incentivization now must compensate for not only wait-and-save but also 

time-shifting when the operator decides to change the time of charging for any EV driver from their preferred option. The optimization variables include the charging rate setpoint, the LQR controller, and a binary variable that decides if a given EV should charge at a given time slot or otherwise. The resulting problem is solved in two stages: the first stage optimizes the charging rates and LQR controllers for the preferred charging times, while the second stage greedily explores feasible time-slot reassignments and re-optimizes the affected variables. In summary, the two primary contributions of this work are: 1) We develop a finite-horizon, greedy-heuristic-based co-optimization and control algorithm for EV charging that optimizes both small-signal dynamic performance and steady-state VSI of a distribution grid while evaluating the right incentives for flexible participation of EV users. 2) We propose a deployment strategy with multiple incen-tive options that accounts for uncertainty in EV user se-lection and evaluates its impact on system performance while enabling users the best possible ways to engage with the proposed scheme. We demonstrate our algorithm using a variant of the IEEE 33-bus radial feeder distribution system model with multiple EVCSs distributed across different buses. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section II recapitulates the nonlinear state-space model of the EVCS-integrated distribution grid. Sections III and IV present the problem formulation and solu-tion strategy of the proposed approach, followed by numerical results in Section V. Section VI concludes the paper. II. Consider a power distribution system with n buses and p

EVCSs. We assume that each EVCS has a fixed capacity and only operates in grid-to-vehicle (G2V) mode. Each EVCS consists of a centralized AC/DC converter with a front-end LCL filter for creating a common DC bus, and DC/DC converters, which act as the charging ports. For more details about the model, please see [9], [10]. The synchronization between the EVCS and the network is ensured by a phase-locked loop (PLL), which provides the synchronous phase angle required for designing the controller of the converter. Each EVCS may have multiple charging ports connected to its respective DC bus, where every charging port has similar internal dynamics [11]. As shown in [9], the dynamics of 3

the charging ports are ignored for simplicity, as the time-scale of those dynamics is significantly faster than that of the dominant oscillations exhibited in typical EV plug-in or plug-out responses of the DC-link voltage of the EVCS. Short-line models are considered for tie-lines between consecutive buses. The operating point of the distribution system model de-pends on which EV is connected to which EVCS, and also on the charging current used for charging the EVs at each EVCS. VI. C ONCLUSIONS 

In this paper, we presented a two-stage optimization frame-work for scheduling the charging of large fleets of EVs in a power distribution grid. Unlike conventional scheduling methods that are usually based on steady-state objectives, our approach ensures improvement of small-signal stability, small-signal dynamic performance, and voltage stability of the dis-tribution system model. This is enabled by an incentivization strategy that encourages EV drivers to shift their preferred time-slots for charging as well as waiting in the queue at the charging station for a few additional minutes in exchange for money. The framework can be especially useful for heavy-duty vehicles such as electric buses and trucks that have more flexibility in terms of charging time while driving on the highway during peak hours. Numerical results show that grid operators can benefit from this approach by enhancing their operating margins during heavy-load conditions.
