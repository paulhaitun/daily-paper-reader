# Reinforcement Learning-Based Pricing and Operation for Hybrid Electric and Hydrogen Vehicle Stations 

Lowell Welburn, Yang Chen, Xudong Wang, Abhilasha Saroj, and Shaked Regev 

Abstract —Electric vehicles (EVs) and hydrogen fuel cell vehi-cles (HFCVs) are promising technologies in the efforts to reduce carbon emissions. Challenges such as limited infrastructure, con-sumer dissatisfaction, and electrical grid strain inhibit progress in the adoption of these technologies. A zero emission vehicle (ZEV) station, capable of handling EVs and HFCVs, addresses these challenges by focusing on maximizing station profit and consumer utility, while minimizing grid impacts. The purpose of this research is to develop a realistic simulation of a ZEV station that is capable of handling real-time pricing decisions enabled by reinforcement learning (RL) and to compare the performance of the RL agent to static pricing methods. After training, the RL agent gains 10.83% greater station profit than the baseline manual parameter search. This work serves as a useful tool for ZEV station owners when handling complex pricing and purchasing decisions. 

Index Terms —Dynamic Pricing, Reinforcement Learning, Zero Emission Vehicle, Multi-Agent Simulation 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Global climate change has been primarily attributed to an increase in carbon emissions over the last century [1]. In 2022, light-, medium-, and heavy-duty vehicles within the transportation industry contributed 22.4% of the total greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions in the US [2]. Many efforts have been made to reduce carbon emissions in recent years, such as the adoption of zero emission vehicles (ZEVs) which include electric vehicles (EVs) and hydrogen fuel cell vehicles (HFCVs). Increasing popularity of ZEVs necessitates an in-creased infrastructure capacity to support these technologies. For this effort to be successful, the additional infrastructure must: 1) be financially viable, 2) maintain consumer trust and satisfaction, and 3) minimize negative impacts to power grid.

Currently, EV users most commonly choose between rel-atively slow AC charging from home and fast DC charging stations abroad. Residential time-of-use (TOU) programs com-monly determine the price of electricity charged by utility providers depending on the time of day to incentivize cus-tomers to consume less electricity during high impact times, reducing strain on the energy grid. HFCV drivers use publicly or privately owned hydrogen fueling stations to refuel their vehicles, where the procedure and time duration is similar to refueling a gasoline vehicle [3]. The possibility of ZEV stations that are capable of handling the recharging and refueling for multiple vehicle fuel types has been explored, but further research is needed to support this effort [4]. ZEV stations benefit from the ability to store and convert hydrogen to electricity or vice versa in times of need. ZEV stations could be more widely adopted to support multiple vehicle fuel types to improve the convenience of the charging and refueling process [5]. Despite the growing need for integrated ZEV stations, exist-ing pricing strategies proposed for real-world deployment by station owners largely rely on static models, traditional game theory, or rule-based heuristics that fail to adapt dynamically to real-time fluctuations in unexpected user demand and grid constraints. This inability to autonomously learn and optimize complex, multi-agent interactions often results in sub-optimal station profitability, increased consumer wait times, and un-necessary strain on the local power grid. Therefore, there is a critical need for an adaptive, data-driven pricing mechanism capable of simultaneously maximizing station profit, maintain-ing consumer utility, and minimizing grid impacts within a hybrid ZEV environment. Many previous works have focused on methods to dynamically set prices for the EV charging case, however, few studies have been performed for dynamic pricing of hybrid EV/HFCV charging/refueling stations where the complex relationships between the two systems are considered. This paper presents the model of a multi-modal ZEV station along with a deep reinforcement learning (DRL) agent effec-tive at making real-time pricing decisions for the ZEV station. The performance of the dynamic DRL agent is compared with manual, single-episode, and TOU static pricing methods. The primary contributions of this work are: 1) Development of a hybrid EV/HFCV multi-agent ZEV station simulation framework. 2) Formulation of a PPO-based real-time pricing and energy management agent. arXiv:2610.23464v1 [cs.CE] 16 Oct 2026 3) Comparison between static, TOU, and fully dynamic RL pricing policies. 4) Analysis of customer utility behaviors including balking and reneging under dynamic pricing. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Traditional Pricing Strategies 

Strategies that do not rely on deep learning have been used in the past and currently for dynamic pricing. Game-theoretical models have been explored where local prosumer behavior and profit distribution were compared when working in coopera-tive and competitive scenarios [6]. A multi-agent simulation (MAS) was presented where the charging station (CS) agent dynamically sets the price using a rule-based strategy built on the probabilistic demand distribution of arriving vehicles [7]. Multiple works have implemented Stackelberg game theo-retic pricing. One of these uses a two-stage framework com-bining LSTM demand forecasting with a bilevel Stackelberg game optimization to determine both the location and pricing of charging stations, where the leader selects locations and followers set prices to maximize payoffs [8]. Another intro-duces a conic relaxation method for solving the non-convex Stackelberg game between profit-maximizing charging station operators and cost-minimizing EV users whose routing choices are influenced by pricing decisions [9]. Strategic interaction between HFCVs and hydrogen refueling stations has also been modeled using a Stackelberg game approach where the pro-posed pricing and refueling strategy simultaneously improves station profit and reduces consumer refueling costs [10]. Sev-eral efforts have also focused on optimization-based pricing techniques [11]–[13]. A bilevel model has been applied where a charging network provider (leader) sets time-varying prices across stations to maximize profit and reduce grid impact, while customers (followers) independently choose charging times subject to those prices [14]. Many of the previously mentioned strategies lack the ability to autonomously adapt to a changing world environment and do not scale well to large multi-agent simulations which is why deep learning methods have been studied recently. 

B. Dynamic Pricing Using Deep Learning 

With the rise of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML), deep learning has been employed is various research efforts using unique approaches to solve the problems with dynamic pricing and negative grid impacts [15]. One such work integrated real-time data with an XGBoost-based algorithm to develop a day-ahead dynamic pricing model to help reduce grid impacts [16]. Another work presented a novel Multi-Agent Deep Neural Network (MADNN) that considers energy balance arrival time, departure time, and range anxiety to determine dynamic pricing of EV fast chargers [17]. A variety of DRL-specific methods have also been imple-mented, helping to solve different aspects of the dynamic charging problem. For example, DRL has been successfully used to help local power grids solve charging scheduling problems to reduce uneven load distribution and provide cus-tomers with lower prices [12], [18]. Narayan et al. presented a method to maximize an aggregator’s revenue based on a Deep Q-Network (DQN) RL agent that has pricing control over multiple EV charging stations throughout the environment [19]. Another study showed the use of DRL to vehicle routing and energy scheduling of emergency EVs during events where the power supply is affected [20]. A novel DRL approach that maximizes the quality of service was also developed and validated [21]. The soft actor-critic (SAC) reinforcement learn-ing algorithm has been implemented to determine the optimal spatial placement and dynamic pricing of EV charging stations [22]. Cui et al. presented a Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) based agent that aimed to maximize charging station profit through dynamic pricing in a competitive scenario built on a traffic flow prediction model [23]. A limited number of studies have focused on a multi-functional charging station for electric and hydrogen vehicles. One such work focuses on the relationship between hydrogen production facilities and the multi-purpose stations that relies on an upper level Stackelberg game approach for hydrogen trading and a lower level asymmetric Nash bargaining game for electricity trading between charging stations [24]. Another study compares multiple DRL algorithms that optimized the retail price of hydrogen for a hydrogen refueling station that also interacts with the electricity grid [25]. III. M ETHODOLOGY 

The present work builds off of prior work research that integrated many dynamic factors such as battery storage, hy-drogen production, hydrogen storage, fuel cells, and dynamic interactions with the electricity grid [26]. The simulation consists of multiple rule-based non-learning EV and HFCV agents and a single DRL agent dynamically making pricing, purchasing, and energy conversion decisions, as shown in Fig. 1. The ZEV station has a battery storage of 2000 kWh and a hydrogen tank storage of 500 kg. For this simulation, energy can be transferred from electricity to hydrogen and vice versa via electrolyzer and fuel cell capabilities, with both having an assumed efficiency of 50% [4]. Each episode runs for 24 hours, with a timestep size of 5 minutes. For this simulation the TOU rates are defined as: 1) discount from 1am-6am, 2) off-peak from 6am-6pm and 9pm-1am, and 3) peak from 6pm-9pm. Fig. 2 shows the concept framework interactions between the DRL agent, physical station environment, and non-learning customer agents.

V. C ONCLUSION 

This paper presented a multi-agent simulation framework for a hybrid ZEV station capable of servicing both EVs and HFCVs. A DRL agent utilizing the PPO algorithm was devel-oped to make dynamic pricing decisions and the performance of the continuous DRL agent was evaluated against manual, static, and TOU pricing strategies. The results demonstrate that the dynamic agent achieved a 10.83% increase in net station profit compared to manual pricing, while simultaneously in-creasing the served customer percentage by +11.02%. Analysis of consumer balking and reneging behaviors highlighted that queue length during peak daytime hours is the primary driver of lost transactions. Future work could benefit from validation against real-world charging station data. Ultimately, this DRL-based dynamic pricing framework provides an autonomous tool for ZEV station operators to maximize profitability, adapt to fluctuating market conditions, and support the broader integration of sustainable transportation infrastructure.
