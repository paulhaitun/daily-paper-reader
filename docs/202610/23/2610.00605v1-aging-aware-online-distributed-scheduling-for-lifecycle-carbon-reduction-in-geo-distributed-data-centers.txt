# Aging-Aware Online Distributed Scheduling for Lifecycle Carbon Reduction in Geo-Distributed Data Centers 

Junyu Lin, Wenjie Liu, Shunbo Lei, Senior Member, IEEE, Wentian Lu, Jianhui Wang, Member, IEEE, Junhong Liu, Member, IEEE 

Abstract—The rapid proliferation of data centers (DCs), driven by cloud computing and artificial intelligence (AI), has led to massive energy demand and carbon emissions, posing significant sustainability challenges. Carbon-aware op-timization in geographically distributed data centers has been widely studied. Most existing approaches mainly focus on operational carbon emissions from server usage. However, ex-isting literature often ignores workload-induced thermal stress, which accelerates nonlinear hardware degradation. This leads to more frequent server replacements and ultimately increases embodied carbon emissions 1. To address these limitations, we propose a comprehensive carbon life-cycle modeling framework for distributed data centers. Apart from operational carbon emissions, this work combines workload scheduling with autilization-dependent exponential aging model to evaluate long-term carbon costs from server degradation. In order to solve the proposed optimization model in an online and privacy-preserving manner, an enhanced Lyapunov framework with time-varying queue shifting (TVQS) is first introduced to han-dle system uncertainties. Then, a zero-sum perturbation-based alternating direction method of multipliers (ZSP-ADMM) framework is developed to enable distributed coordination across geographically separated data centers while protecting locally exchanged workload information. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves up to 13.0% lower carbon emissions and 12.6% lower operational costs compared with benchmarks. Index Terms—Data center, carbon-aware, hardware degra-dation, online algorithm, distributed mechanism.

These trends raise significant concerns regarding electricity costs and environmental sustainabil-ity, making cost-efficient and carbon-aware operation an imperative research priority [3]. To address these challenges, extensive research efforts have focused on real-time carbon-aware operational man-agement in geographically distributed data centers [2]. Subsequently, research pivoted toward op-erational flexibility, leveraging geographical load balancing and Lyapunov-based online scheduling to exploit spatio-temporal variations in carbon intensity and renewable energy availability [5], [6], [7]. These concepts have reached industrial maturity through Google’s carbon-intelligent system using Virtual Capacity Curves [8], with further refinements extending to dual-dimension task migration and edge-computing resource allocation [9], [10]. Field data from large-scale data centers indicate that regular hardware replacement is unavoidable due to inevitable component wear and failure [15]. Such replacement re-quires continuous manufacturing of new devices and dis-posal of retired equipment. In particular, manufacturing high-performance components such as solid-state drives (SSDs) generates substantial embodied carbon emissions [16]. Although these manufacturing and disposal emissions account for over half of the total lifecycle carbon footprint in modern computing [17], they are rarely integrated into dynamic data center scheduling. Most existing approaches, including carbon-aware work-load scheduling methods [18], [19], [20] and cloud-edge lifecycle management frameworks [21], focus primarily on energy efficiency or operational carbon emissions. They overlook heterogeneous degradation caused by varying server utilization levels. This oversight leads to underes-timation of the actual lifecycle carbon footprint [22], [23]. Although a recent study [24] considers both operational and embodied carbon, its embodied carbon accounting is primarily based on active server counts and accumulated arXiv:2610.00605v1 [eess.SY] 30 Sep 2026 2

operating time, without explicitly modeling utilization-dependent hardware degradation. Consequently, it may underestimate the embodied carbon emissions associated with accelerated hardware degradation under high uti-lization. Moreover, its offline scheduling method relies on future information about workload arrivals, renewable generation, and electricity prices, while its centralized coordination raises privacy concerns through information exchange among data centers. Lyapunov optimization has been widely applied to online data center scheduling, including the joint coor-dination of computing workloads and battery energy [25], electricity procurement under renewable generation and price uncertainties [26], and cluster energy minimization under data redundancy constraints [7]. However, standard Lyapunov frameworks rely on static virtual queue shift parameters, preventing energy storage from adaptively responding to real-time fluctuations in renewable gener-ation and electricity prices. On the other hand, alter-nating direction method of multipliers (ADMM)-based algorithms have been widely applied to distributed data center coordination, including computational-task coordi-nation among multiple data center operators [27] and joint workload and energy scheduling across regional peer-to-peer energy markets [28], [29]. However, exchanging partial operational data in these ADMM schemes still exposes workload patterns and user privacy. To address the aforementioned modeling and algo-rithmic limitations, this paper proposes a low-carbon online distributed operation framework for geographically distributed data centers. The main contributions are summarized as follows: 1) We develop a lifecycle carbon model for geo-distributed data centers that jointly accounts for op-erational carbon emissions and hardware-aging-induced embodied carbon emissions. The model links cluster-level server utilization to nonlinear lifetime degradation and calculates the resulting embodied carbon emissions. Simulation results show that the proposed framework reduces total carbon emissions by 13.0% and operational costs by 12.6% relative to the benchmark. 2) We formulate a long-term optimization problem that jointly minimizes economic costs and lifecycle carbon emis-sions, and develop a Lyapunov-based online method that transforms it into per-slot subproblems without requiring future system information. A time-varying queue shifting (TVQS) mechanism adjusts the battery virtual-queue shift according to real-time electricity prices and renewable generation. Simulation results show that the proposed mechanism enables the battery to adapt its charging and discharging decisions to variations in electricity prices and renewable availability. 3) We propose a zero-sum perturbation ADMM (ZSP-ADMM) framework to enable distributed coordination across geographically separated data centers without di-rectly exposing their local workload migration decisions. A structured perturbation mechanism is incorporated into the ADMM information exchange, where coordinated zero-sum perturbations preserve the global workload-balance structure while obfuscating local migration sig-nals. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed ZSP-ADMM achieves effective workload masking with negligible computational overhead. II. Proposed DC Operation Framework

III. Solution Method Directly solving P1 in a centralized offline manner is impractical due to two fundamental structural challenges: First, problem P1 exhibits strong inter-temporal cou-pling across scheduling horizons through physical state transitions, including (15), (24), and (29). Offline opti-mization over the operating horizon requires prior knowl-edge of future renewable generation, electricity prices, and workload arrivals, which are uncertain. To address these temporal dependencies without future predictions, we design an online Lyapunov optimization framework to decouple the long-term problem into per-slot subproblems. Second, the workload-balance constraint in (12) couples migration decisions across data centers. A centralized solution requires sharing local operational data, raising privacy concerns. We therefore develop a zero-sum pertur-bation ADMM (ZSP-ADMM) algorithm that decomposes the problem into local subproblems. Structured zero-sum perturbations are applied to the exchanged migration signals, masking local decisions while preserving the global workload balance. A. Conclusion This paper proposes an aging-aware online distributed scheduling framework for geo-distributed data centers. The model incorporates utilization-dependent hardware aging into lifecycle carbon accounting. A Lyapunov-based method with TVQS enables online scheduling and adaptive battery management, while ZSP-ADMM masks migration volumes during distributed coordination. Sim-ulation results yield the following findings: 1) Combining workload migration with aging-aware scheduling reduces economic costs and lifecycle carbon emissions while pre-serving hardware health by prioritizing clusters with lower aging rates. 2) The proposed Lyapunov-based strategy with TVQS reduces conservative battery utilization by adapting charging and discharging decisions to real-time renewable generation and electricity prices. 3) Zero-sum perturbations mask local migration decisions while pre-serving global workload balance, with little change in the ZSP-ADMM convergence behavior.
