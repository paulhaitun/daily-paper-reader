# Joint Freshness and Deadline Feasibility in Store-and-Forward Satellite IoT Networks. 

S¨ uleyman C ¸ ıtır 

Middle East Technical University Ankara, T¨ urkiye suleyman.citir@metu.edu.tr 

Abstract —3GPP Release 19 supports store-and-forward satel-lite access, where traffic accumulated during feeder-link outages must be forwarded through finite, geometry-limited windows. Static priority levels offer little discrimination among large backlogs and ignore the decay in packet value. We formulate QoS feasibility for status updates, measured by destination freshness, and deadline-constrained event reports over periodic two-hop contacts with finite onboard storage. For general calendars, we derive ordering-sensitive miss, cohort-capacity, delivery-rate, and freshness bounds, including an exact status-only characterization. For single status flow and single event class sharing a contact pair per period, a finite-state program gives exact joint feasibility for arbitrary integer age thresholds. Numerical results quantify tradeoffs among load, capacity, deadline, and freshness tolerance. 

Index Terms —Age of information, QoS, Internet of Things, satellite communications, scheduling, store-and-forward. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Store-and-forward operation allows a satellite to collect traffic while its feeder link is unavailable and deliver it when ground connectivity returns. Release 19 supports this mode for satellite access in E-UTRAN [1]. For satellite IoT, however, accepting a packet onboard is only the first step toward useful delivery. Its remaining lifetime must accommodate both the wait for a feeder contact and competition for the capacity of that contact. Different applications place different demands on these opportunities. For routine monitoring, a new status update may replace an older one; what matters is how current the destination’s information remains, as measured by age of information (AoI) [2]. An event report, such as a fault alarm, is a distinct message whose delivery must precede a deadline. Forwarding more status updates can improve freshness while leaving fewer opportunities for events. Conversely, postponing a refresh can release capacity for events, provided that the freshness contract allows it. Before choosing a scheduler, an operator therefore needs to know whether the two commitments can be met together. Average capacity does not settle this question. An event must encounter a service contact followed by a feeder contact before its deadline, and the resulting opportunities must also support the required refreshes. Even separate freshness and event-delivery tests can pass when their joint requirements are infeasible. We study feasibility under periodic contacts and finite on-board storage, with freshness measured by stale-slot fractions and event service by deadline-miss fractions. Theorem 1 gives the exact status-only stale-slot floor and an attaining policy when resources suffice. For mixed traffic, Lemma 2 reduces the specified contact-pair model losslessly, and Theorem 2 gives an exact finite-program test for joint feasibility. The phase, cohort-capacity, and aggregate-rate bounds are support-ing necessary tests for general calendars. II. R ELATED WORK 

Predictable contacts are a central resource in disruption-tolerant satellite networks. Fraire and Finochietto [3] dis-cuss contact-plan design, including the role of routing and traffic information. More directly, Arı et al. [4] study peak-AoI minimization under intermittent satellite connectivity and store-and-forward delays, comparing different levels of contact awareness under a transmission budget. Mixed freshness and deadline-constrained traffic has also been studied directly. Sun et al. [5] schedule status updates alongside timely-throughput traffic over a shared wireless link, seeking to minimize weighted average AoI subject to timely-throughput constraints. Fountoulakis et al. [6] consider an AoI-oriented user and a deadline-constrained user sharing an error-prone channel; they formulate average-AoI minimization with timely- throughput constraints as a constrained Markov decision process (CMDP) and develop dynamic-programming-based policies. Thus, neither the coexistence of these traffic types nor CMDP modeling is claimed as a contribution here. Freshness scheduling over multiple hops is studied by Bedewy et al. [7], who establish age-optimality results for last-generated-first-served policies under specified service assump-tions. Li et al. [8] examine competition between AoI and ser-vice delay in mobile edge caching through achievable-region bounds and a drift-plus-penalty algorithm. Wang et al. [9] de-velop statistical peak-AoI, delay, and error-rate guarantees for satellite–terrestrial networks in the finite-blocklength regime. These works address related freshness, delay, or contact ef-fects, but not the joint feasibility problem under the contact and contract structure considered here. III. A. Network and Contact Calendar 

We consider an uplink store-and-forward (S&F) network of the kind standardized for satellite access in 3GPP Re-lease 19 [1]: multiple ground sources, one satellite with arXiv:2610.27966v1 [cs.NI] 20 Oct 2026 onboard storage, and a ground application reached through a gateway. Each packet traverses a source-to-satellite service link and then a satellite-to-ground feeder link, so onboard reception does not constitute delivery. Time is divided into slots of duration τ , indexed by t ∈ Z≥0.The contact calendar repeats every P slots. All sources share a service window of S slots per period. The feeder window has duration w and starts at phase ϕ, where 

1 ≤ S ≤ P, 0 ≤ w ≤ P, ϕ ∈ { 0, . . . , P − 1}.

All calendar parameters are integers. Choosing the service-window opening as phase zero, the link-availability indicators are 

a(t) = 1{t mod P < S },bw(t) = 1{(t − ϕ) mod P < w }. (1) The modulo operation takes values in {0, . . . , P − 1}, so a feeder window may cross a period boundary. An available service or feeder slot supports at most cs or cf equal-sized packets, respectively, both positive integers. The links have separate capacities and operate concurrently: a packet may be collected while a different packet is forwarded in the same slot. Each hop occupies one slot, which absorbs its transmission and propagation time. A packet collected in slot t therefore becomes available onboard at t + 1 and cannot be forwarded in slot t.

This section develops necessary conditions for the fixed-QoS feasible region in (10). Three mechanisms restrict it. First, the order of the service and feeder windows inside a period decides which arrival phases admit a deadline-feasible path at all, and, for status traffic, exactly how stale the destination must become; on the status-only slice this yields a characterization when both link capacities and onboard storage can support one packet per flow simultaneously. Second, even at phases that admit a path, only a bounded number of packets can traverse the ordered contacts within one deadline. Third, the QoS contracts impose a minimum successful delivery rate, which the link capacities must support. All three apply to every admissible policy, independently of its scheduling rule.
