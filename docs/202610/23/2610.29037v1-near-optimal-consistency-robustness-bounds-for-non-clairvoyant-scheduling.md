## 摘要
我们研究在具有预测处理时间的单机非预知调度中刻画最优一致性—鲁棒性权衡的问题。对于固定的作业数 n，Wei 和 Zhang（2020）建立了可实现的一致性—鲁棒性权衡的一个下界；已知该下界仅在少数限制性情形下是紧的，这使得一般 n 下的最优权衡仍未解决。Benomar 等人（2025）引入了另一种最坏 n 情形，他们利用前述工作的构造表明：对于 λ∈(0,1)，任何 (1+λ)-一致算法都必须是 1+Ω(√(1/λ))-鲁棒的。另一方面，已知最好的上界给出 (1+λ)-一致性和 1+O(1/λ)-鲁棒性，从而留下一个多项式间隙。在本工作中，我们利用多阶段对抗性构造开发了一个新的下界，并提出了一个新算法；二者共同给出了一致性—鲁棒性权衡的近乎最优刻画：对于每个 n，我们的上界与下界相差不超过乘法因子 3/2，因此我们的算法与在线最优相差不超过乘法因子 3/2。特别地，我们的下界表明：当 n→∞ 时，任何 (1+λ)-一致算法的鲁棒性至少为 1/√λ；而我们的算法对于任意 n 都能达到至多 1/2+√(5/4+1/λ) 的鲁棒性，并在 λ↓0 时与下界的首项匹配；我们的上界也填补了最坏 n 情形中的多项式间隙。我们还进一步说明如何使我们的算法具有容错性，在有界预测误差下仅造成性能略微下降，同时保持近乎最优的一致性—鲁棒性权衡。

## Abstract
We study the problem of characterizing the optimal consistency--robustness tradeoff for single-machine non-clairvoyant scheduling with predicted processing times. For a fixed number of jobs $n$, Wei and Zhang (2020) established a lower bound on the achievable consistency--robustness tradeoff which is known to be tight only in a few restrictive cases, leaving the optimal tradeoff for general $n$ unresolved. A different worst-case-$n$ regime was introduced by Benomar et al. (2025), who applied the former work's construction to show that for $λ\in (0, 1)$, any $(1+λ)$-consistent algorithm must be $1 + Ω(\sqrt{1/λ})$-robust. On the other hand, the best known upper bound gives $(1+λ)$-consistency and $1+O(1/λ)$-robustness, leaving a polynomial gap. In this work, we develop a new lower bound using a multi-stage adversarial construction and a new algorithm that together give a near-optimal characterization of the consistency--robustness tradeoff: for every $n$, our upper bound is within a multiplicative factor $3/2$ of our lower bound, and thus our algorithm is within a multiplicative factor $3/2$ of the online optimal. In particular, our lower bound shows that any $(1+λ)$-consistent algorithm must have robustness at least $1/\sqrtλ$ as $n \to \infty$, while our algorithm achieves robustness at most $\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{5}{4}+1/λ}$ for any $n$, matching the lower bound in the leading term as $λ\downarrow 0$; our upper bound also closes the polynomial gap in the worst-case-$n$ regime. We additionally show how our algorithm can be made error-tolerant, preserving the near-optimal consistency-robustness tradeoff while incurring only slightly degraded performance under bounded prediction errors.
