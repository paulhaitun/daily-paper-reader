## 摘要
人工智能工作负载的快速增长正在加速数据中心的扩张与电网互联。现有数据中心负载接纳（DCLA）评估主要关注电网与资源充裕性，并往往意味着更大的工作负载灵活性总会提高可接纳容量。然而，工作负载灵活性具有双重效应。工作负载延迟与时移可以缓解网络拥塞，而训练工作负载的同步检查点会造成大幅且快速的负荷下降，威胁频率稳定性。因此，DCLA不能仅由电网充裕性决定。本文提出一种最优DCLA方法，联合考虑工作负载服务质量（QoS）、检查点诱发频率安全（FS）与电网充裕性。首先，建立QoS感知模型，刻画依赖于工作负载的灵活性，同时确保服务可行性。其次，针对周期性与事件触发功率波动，建立检查点诱发FS约束，并将关键频率安全限值重述为代数形式。第三，建立两阶段字典序模型，以最大化DCLA并最小化所需储能（ES）部署，然后在给定的数据中心与ES选址定容决策下优化系统运行。案例研究表明，与电网充裕性基准相比，所提方法避免了40.3%的DCLA高估。QoS是主要限制因素，而随着训练工作负载增加，检查点诱发频率安全约束变得更加严格。这些发现突出表明，可靠的DCLA应同时考虑其电网支撑价值以及相关的QoS与FS风险。

## Abstract
The rapid growth of artificial intelligence workloads is accelerating the expansion and grid interconnection of data centers (DCs). Existing data center load accommodation (DCLA) assessments primarily focus on grid and resource adequacy and often imply that greater workload flexibility always increases accommodable capacity. However, workload flexibility has a dual effect. Workload deferral and temporal shifting can alleviate network congestion, whereas synchronized checkpointing of training workloads can cause large and rapid load reductions, threatening frequency stability. Therefore, DCLA cannot be determined solely by grid adequacy. This paper develops an optimal DCLA method that jointly accounts for workload quality of service (QoS), checkpoint-induced frequency security (FS), and grid adequacy. First, a QoS-aware model characterizes workload-dependent flexibility while ensuring service feasibility. Second, checkpoint-induced FS constraints are developed for periodic and event-triggered power variations, with key frequency-security limits reformulated in algebraic form. Third, a two-stage lexicographic model is developed to maximize DCLA and minimizes the required energy storage (ES) deployment, and then optimizes system operation under the given DC and ES siting and sizing decisions. Case studies show that the proposed method avoids a 40.3% overestimation of DCLA compared with the grid-adequacy benchmark. QoS is the primary limiting factor, while checkpoint-induced frequency-security constraints become more restrictive as training workloads increase. These findings highlight that reliable DCLA should account for both its grid-support value and the associated QoS and FS risks.
