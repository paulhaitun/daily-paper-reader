## 摘要
当智能体具有私有的、随机且可排队等待一段时间需求，并且可能采取策略性甚至对抗性行为时，如何在不用货币的情况下分配共享资源？我们提出一种非货币在线资源配置范式，称为采样成对外部性补偿器（Sampled Pairwise Externality Compensator, SPEC）。我们的目标是保证每个智能体获得其理想效用的大部分：理想效用是一个个体基准，对应于在容量均分所允许的范围内，服务其需求中价值最高的部分。我们寻求无论其他智能体行为如何（包括潜在对抗性和串谋行为）都能成立的保证。我们的方法基于成对外部性补偿的思想，通过用采样估计替代对未来外部性的精确计算，使其计算高效。核心洞见在于，通过耦合模拟所利用的局部混合，可以高效计算这些估计，同时保持稳健的效用保证。在我们的主要技术贡献中，我们将 SPEC 实例化到一个随机调度模型中：智能体收到具有私有价值的作业，未服务的作业跨期持续存在并受随机过期影响，单个服务器每期至多处理一个作业。我们的机制为每个智能体提供一个简单策略，该策略可实现完全知情规划者所能提供的理想效用的最优最坏情况比例，直至总效用中的次线性加性损失。我们还表明，当过期概率趋于零时，该比例在长时域极限下趋近于 1：足够持久的作业使每个智能体能够获得几乎其全部理想效用。该机制每期以 O(n^2) 期望摊还时间运行，其中 n 是智能体数量；值得注意的是，每期的期望摊还计算开销与时间时域 T 无关。

## Abstract
How can a shared resource be allocated without money when agents have private, stochastic demands that can be queued for a while, and may behave strategically, or even adversarially? We introduce a paradigm for non-monetary online resource allocation called Sampled Pairwise Externality Compensator (SPEC). Our objective is to guarantee each agent a large fraction of its ideal utility: an individual benchmark corresponding to serving the highest-valued fraction of its demand permitted by an equal share of capacity. We seek guarantees that hold regardless of other agents' behavior, including potentially adversarial and collusive behavior. Our approach builds on the idea of pairwise externality compensation, making it computationally efficient by replacing exact calculations of future externalities with sampled estimates. The central insight is that local mixing, exploited through coupled simulations, allows these estimates to be computed efficiently while preserving robust utility guarantees. In our main technical contribution, we instantiate SPEC in a stochastic scheduling model in which agents receive jobs with private values, unserved jobs persist across periods subject to random expiration, and a single server processes at most one job per period. Our mechanism gives each agent a simple strategy that achieves the optimal worst-case fraction of ideal utility available to a fully informed planner, up to a sublinear additive loss in total utility. We also show that this fraction approaches $1$ in the long-horizon limit as the expiration probability vanishes: sufficiently persistent jobs allow each agent to secure nearly its full ideal utility. The mechanism runs in $O(n^2)$ expected amortized time per period, where $n$ is the number of agents; significantly, the expected amortized computational overhead per period is independent of the time horizon $T$.
