# Optimal Dynamic Resource Allocation for Multicore Real-time Systems 

Georgiy A. Bondar, Abigail Eisenklam, Ripon C. Sarker, Linh Thi Xuan Phan, Abhishek Halder 

Abstract — We formulate a novel optimal control problem for data-driven dynamic resource allocation in multicore real-time systems. The proposed formulation accounts for hard deadline constraints and resource bounds. Under mild assumptions, we prove several results on the feasibility and existence of optimal solution for the proposed formulation. Building on these analyses, we design an algorithm to solve the multi-core dynamic resource allocation problem. We demonstrate the practical use of this new algorithm by experimentally evaluating it on a real multicore platform using four benchmarks from the literature. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Motivation. Real-time systems, such as those in automotive, robotic, and avionic applications, require predictable timing to operate safely. To meet growing computational demands, these systems are increasingly deployed on multicore plat-forms, where cores share hardware resources such as the last-level cache (LLC) and memory bus. Resource contention arising from this sharing can cause interference among concurrently executing programs, increasing their execution times and, in the worst case, causing deadline misses. A common remedy is to statically partition shared resources across cores [1], [2], typically by solving amixed-integer linear program (MILP) or an approximation thereof [3], [4]. Because these allocations are static, they must be provisioned for the worst-case demand, even though a program’s resource demand may vary over time. Dynamic allocation addresses this limitation by redistributing shared-resource partitions across cores at runtime in response to changing demand. Existing dynamic approaches [5], how-ever, rely on greedy heuristics [6], [7]. While resource allo-cation for soft deadline constraints has been studied before [8], to the best of our knowledge, no prior work computes 

optimal dynamic allocations subject to hard deadlines. In this work, we formulate dynamic multicore resource allocation as a continuous-time continuous-state optimal control problem (OCP) subject to hard deadline constraints. The objective of our OCP captures a system-level cost, such as energy consumption or provisioned resources, which is critical for embedded platforms with stringent size, weight, and power (SWaP) constraints. and satisfies (7), we now present our approach to the solution of problem (2) as Algorithm 1 which takes us from empirical data on task resource usage to a locally optimal allocation on a given taskset. The proposed Algorithm 1 first fits true and under-approximated dynamics to each task, then finds the opti-mal allocation for the underestimated dynamics (a finite-dimensional, convex program with the assumption on J, and yielding a piecewise-constant allocation as the optimum, by Theorem 2). A. Evaluation Setup 

Programs. Our case study uses benchmarks from the San Diego Vision Benchmark Suite (SD-VBS) [14], following prior work on multicore resource allocation [4]. Table I summarizes the benchmarks. Each benchmark was profiled and executed using the vga input class from SD-VBS. 

Platform. We performed all measurements and ran all exper-iments on an Intel Xeon E5-2618L v3 processor with 8 cores, a 20 MB 20-way set associative last-level L3 cache, and a single-channel 8 GB PC-2133 DDR4 DRAM. We consider two shared resources: the LLC and memory bandwidth, each divided into 20 partitions using Intel’s Cache Allocation Technology [2] and MemGuard [1], respectively. Because the Cache Allocation Technology enforces a minimum allocation of 2 partitions per core on our platform, the set of feasible allocations, prior to normalization, is 

Rknown := {2, 3, . . . , 20 } × { 1, 2, . . . , 20 }.

To collect these data, we pin the benchmark to a single core, assign it a feasible resource vector r, and sample its state xi(t) every 10 ms. We repeat this procedure five times for each possible resource allocation r, and then compute the corresponding state derivatives ˙xi(t) from the collected time-series data. This yields a dataset of input-output pairs  (xi(t), r ), ˙xi(t)

C. Empirical Evaluation Results 

We next validate on our hardware platform that the computed schedule preserves schedulability in practice and compare its energy consumption against a static baseline. We consider a baseline allocation that statically allocates the full resource capacity of each shared resource evenly across cores. We execute the J1 and J2-optimal schedules and the baseline on our hardware platform, and measure their energy consump-tion and schedulability over the system window. 

Results. The results show that all tasks indeed meet their deadlines when executing on the real platform under both the 

J1 and J2 schedules. The J2-optimal schedule reduces the required number of cache and memory bandwidth partitions by 60% and 15%, respectively, relative to the full capacity of 20 partitions per resource. Further, the J1-optimal schedule reduces the controllable energy consumption (energy con-sumed beyond the static floor) by 6.3% compared to the baseline. These results demonstrate that our OCP formulation and solver can meaningfully optimize system-level objectives via dynamic resource allocation while guaranteeing timing predictability. VI. C ONCLUDING REMARKS 

We presented an optimal control approach for dynamic shared-resource allocation in multicore real-time systems with hard deadlines. By modeling task progress as controlled dynamics and optimizing time-varying resource allocations, our approach reduces both provisioned resource capacity and energy consumption while guaranteeing deadline feasibility. Future work will extend the approach to multiple tasks per core and incorporate online adaptation to account for poten-tial discrepancies between profiled and actual task dynamics at runtime.
