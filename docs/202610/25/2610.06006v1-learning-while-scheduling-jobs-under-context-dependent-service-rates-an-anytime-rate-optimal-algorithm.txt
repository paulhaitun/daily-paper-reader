Abstract 

We study contextual queueing bandits, where a learner schedules jobs while learning unknown service rates modeled by logistic functions of job-server features. Performance is measured by queue length regret, the expected excess queue length at round t relative to an oracle that knows the service rates. Existing decaying-regret guarantees either have a suboptimal decay rate or require a known fixed horizon. They also assume context-wise slack and a strictly positive minimum eigenvalue of the feature covariance. In this paper, we propose WISE (Widest Interval Selection with Elimination), achieving rate-optimal ˜O(t−1/2) queue length regret at every sufficiently large time without knowing the horizon. We assume capacity slack, meaning that expected incoming workload under best-server service is below service capacity, and impose no covariance lower bound. Our analysis uses a workload potential measuring the expected service attempts needed by waiting jobs on their best servers. Its drift on nonempty rounds combines a negative term ensured by capacity slack with errors from suboptimal service choices. Then an elliptical potential count bounds how often WISE selects wide confidence intervals, thereby limiting the number of rounds with large service errors. We also sharpen the arrival-rate dependence of an existing lower bound and make its dependence on feature dimension and server count explicit. We prove another lower bound that quantifies the increase in regret as the normalized capacity slack decreases; to our knowledge, this is the first such lower bound for CQB. Simulations show small regret even when context-wise slack fails. 

# 1 Introduction 

Queueing systems play an important role in cloud computing (Vilaplana et al., 2014), call centers (Koole and Mandelbaum, 2002), and services using large language models (LLMs) (Lee et al., 2024a; Mitzenmacher and Shahout, 2025). Scheduling in these systems requires accounting for two characteristics. First, service rates are often difficult to predict a priori and estimated from observed service outcomes. Second, jobs have different contexts, and the service rate of a job depends on both the job itself and the server assigned to it. For example, an LLM’s probability of successfully answering a query depends on the query’s specifications and the model’s capabilities. Queueing bandits provide a framework for learning unknown service rates while scheduling jobs (Krishnasamy et al., 2021; Choudhury et al., 2021; Stahlbuhk et al., 2021). Scheduling decisions determine both which jobs are served and which service outcomes are observed to estimate these rates. Performance is measured by queue length regret , the expected excess queue length relative 1arXiv:2610.06006v1 [cs.LG] 5 Oct 2026 to an oracle that knows the service rates. A (queue length) regret bound that converges to zero implies that the learner’s expected queue length approaches that of the oracle. Contextual queueing bandits (CQB) extend this framework to incorporate job-specific contexts (Bae et al., 2026a). Earlier work on queueing bandits considers context-independent service rates (Krishnasamy et al., 2021) or a fixed set of queue features (Kim and Oh, 2024). In contrast, CQB allows each job to have its own feature vector. The departure rate is assumed to follow a logistic model of job-server features (Bae et al., 2026a), as in logistic bandits (Filippi et al., 2010; Faury et al., 2020; Jun et al., 2021). This allows scheduling decisions to reflect heterogeneous jobs and servers. Bae et al. (2026a) establish the first decaying queue length regret bound for CQB, of order 

> ˜

O(t−1/4). Bae et al. (2026b) obtain an anytime regret bound of the same order. Bae and Lee (2026a) improve the rate to ˜O(t−1/2), which is optimal up to logarithmic factors, for a fixed horizon 

t known in advance. However, the earlier results on CQB have the following three limitations. First, they either have a suboptimal rate (Bae et al., 2026a,b) or require advance knowledge of the horizon (Bae et al., 2026a; Bae and Lee, 2026a). Cloud and LLM services operate over an indefinite horizon and must maintain stable queues throughout operation, motivating an anytime horizon-free algorithm. Second, all three results require a context-wise slack condition (Bae et al., 2026a,b; Bae and Lee, 2026a): the best service rate exceeds the arrival rate by a uniform positive gap for every context, regardless of how frequently it arrives. A natural relaxation is to require capacity slack : the expected incoming workload under best-server service is below the system’s service capacity. Third, the results also require that the minimum eigenvalue of the feature covariance is strictly positive. This condition fails when similar jobs have features concentrated in a lower-dimensional subspace. Motivated by these limitations, we develop an anytime horizon-free algorithm with a rate-optimal regret bound under minimal assumptions. Instead of a context-wise slack condition, we assume capacity slack stated in Assumption 2. Moreover, the result does not require a positive lower bound on the minimum eigenvalue of the feature covariance. Our contributions are summarized as follows. • We propose WISE (Widest Interval Selection with Elimination), which eliminates job-server pairs that cannot be optimal and selects the widest remaining confidence interval. It achieves anytime horizon-free queue length regret of order ˜O(t−1/2) without knowing the normalized capacity slack ϵ. The guarantee holds under capacity slack without a covariance lower bound, allowing both difficult job contexts and features confined to a lower-dimensional subspace. • We refine the Ω( λt −1/2) lower bound of Bae and Lee (2026a) by proving a round of order Ω(min {λ, min {d, log K}√λ/t }). This sharpens the dependence on the arrival rate λ and makes the dependence on the feature dimension d and the number of servers K explicit. • We prove another lower bound of order Ω(min {ϵ−2, min {d, log K}/(ϵ2√t)}). To our knowledge, this is the first lower bound for CQB that captures the increase in regret as the normalized capacity slack ϵ decreases. This quantifies how service errors persisting for Θ( ϵ−2) rounds increase the difficulty of learning while scheduling. • We compare WISE with seven baselines in simulations with capacity slack, including settings that violate context-wise slack. Varying slow-job service probabilities and the normalized capacity slack shows how these conditions affect queue length regret. WISE attains small regret even when some contexts violate context-wise slack. 2Our analysis for WISE uses two main ideas. First, we replace queue length with a workload potential based on the expected service attempts needed by waiting jobs on their best servers (Gamarnik and Stolyar, 2012). On nonempty rounds, capacity slack gives a negative workload drift term plus an error term from suboptimal service choices, even when queue length itself has positive drift. Second, under valid confidence bounds, WISE’s elimination and widest interval selection link large departure rate gaps to wide selected intervals. The elliptical potential count (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011; Kim et al., 2022) limits such selections without a covariance lower bound. Combined with workload drift, this makes long periods without an empty queue unlikely. When a new job arrives after an empty round, WISE serves it using the most uncertain candidate server. We use these observations to bound uncertainty under the arrival distribution, then combine recent empty rounds with confidence interval intersections to control the expected departure rate gap at each sufficiently large time.

Queueing systems and learning. Queueing systems model service allocation in call centers and computing platforms (Koole and Mandelbaum, 2002; Vilaplana et al., 2014). Queueing bandits address scheduling when service rates are unknown and must be learned from service outcomes (Krishnasamy et al., 2021; Choudhury et al., 2021; Stahlbuhk et al., 2021). Their guarantees include queue length regret at a given time (Krishnasamy et al., 2021), cumulative queue length or holding-cost regret (Stahlbuhk et al., 2021; Krishnasamy et al., 2018), and the maximum increase in time-averaged queue length due to learning (Freund et al., 2023). The ˜O(t−1) guarantee of Krishnasamy et al. (2021) depends on a fixed difference between the best and second-best service rates, whereas our lower bounds allow this difference to decrease with t. Contextual extensions assign a fixed feature to each queue (Kim and Oh, 2024) or allow heterogeneous contexts for individual jobs (Bae et al., 2026a,b; Bae and Lee, 2026a). For the latter setting, existing bounds decay as ˜O(t−1/4) without horizon knowledge (Bae et al., 2026b) or as ˜O(t−1/2) with a known horizon (Bae and Lee, 2026a). These results require context-wise slack and a covariance lower bound. We replace context-wise slack by capacity slack and remove the covariance lower bound. Other learning approaches for queueing systems study MaxWeight scheduling and stability under unknown service rates (Neely et al., 2012; Stahlbuhk et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2023; Wijewardena et al., 2025), scheduling with predicted service times or job classes (Mitzenmacher, 2019; Scully et al., 2021; Lee et al., 2024a; Mitzenmacher and Shahout, 2025), and learning when to defer decisions to congested human reviewers (Lykouris and Weng, 2024). 

Logistic bandits. Our service model connects CQB to logistic bandits, which model binary feedback through a logistic function of action features and an unknown parameter. Following the generalized linear bandit formulation of Filippi et al. (2010), subsequent work improves the dependence on curvature and derives instance-dependent regret bounds (Faury et al., 2020; Abeille et al., 2021; Lee et al., 2024b). Related directions include sharper confidence bounds for finite-action problems (Li et al., 2017; Jun et al., 2021), computationally efficient updates (Faury et al., 2022; Zhang and Sugiyama, 2023), and Thompson sampling (Dong et al., 2019; Kim et al., 2023). Bae and Lee (2026b) remove the assumption of a positive lower bound on the minimum eigenvalue of the context covariance matrix. These works study cumulative reward regret, whereas our analysis requires a bound on the expected departure rate gap at each specified time. 

Elimination algorithms. Elimination algorithms use confidence bounds to discard actions that cannot be optimal. In Auer’s framework, exploration selects a remaining action whose confidence width exceeds a prescribed threshold (Auer, 2002; Chu et al., 2011; Li et al., 2017; Jun et al., 2021). Related methods for Gaussian process optimization select the action with the largest posterior variance, either in combination with UCB (Contal et al., 2013) or after eliminating suboptimal actions (Cai et al., 2021; Li and Scarlett, 2022; Iwazaki and Takeno, 2025; Ma et al., 2026). Confidence interval intersections also appear in level-set estimation and safe optimization (Gotovos, 2013; Sui et al., 2015).
