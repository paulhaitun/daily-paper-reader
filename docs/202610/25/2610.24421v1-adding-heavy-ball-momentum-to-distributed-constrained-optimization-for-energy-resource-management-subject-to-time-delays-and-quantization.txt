Abstract 

This paper studies a hybrid discrete-time signed gradient-consensus method with heavy-ball momentum, paired communication delays, and fixed loga-rithmic quantization. The method preserves the aggregate supply-demand equality at every iteration. For a static connected undirected graph and bounded delays, a Lyapunov-Krasovskii analysis establishes practical stabil-ity by isolating the discontinuous quantization error as an additive distur-bance and closing a computable invariant-sublevel argument. The resulting ergodic residual bound is quadratic in the quantization resolution, and exact convergence to the smooth penalized equality-constrained optimizer is recov-ered in the unquantized case. For delay-free unquantized quadratic costs, an exact modal analysis identifies the heavy-ball stability region and optimal parameters, with the predicted rate validated on a fixed sparse network. A reproducible delayed experiment reports all constants and a certificate whose guaranteed steady-state level lies below the reported initialization. A sepa-rate mixed generator-battery experiment exercises the signed update directly. Finite smooth penalties approximate, but do not generally enforce, the local box constraints exactly. 

Keywords: Distributed constrained optimization, energy resource scheduling, heavy-ball momentum, quantization, time-delays

1. Introduction 

Distributed energy resource management systems (DERMS) coordinate distributed energy resources, including solar generation, storage, electric ve-hicles, flexible loads, and small generators, to satisfy system-level operating objectives [1, 2]. In the optimization model studied here, each resource is represented by a local controller that evaluates a private cost gradient and ex-changes only a quantized signed-gradient value with neighboring controllers. The controllers collectively minimize the separable operating cost while main-taining the aggregate supply-demand equality. Thus, the DERMS interpreta-tion is a networked economic-dispatch/resource-allocation layer rather than a complete model of distribution-system power flow, voltage regulation, or market clearing. Distributing the computation can improve scalability, reduce communi-cation bottlenecks, and remove dependence on a single computational co-ordinator [3]. It does not, by itself, eliminate every single point of failure: communication cut sets, common infrastructure, and critical agents can still fail. Resilience therefore depends on the network topology and implementa-tion. Parallel hardware and distributed scheduling can exploit resources more efficiently than centralized processing [4, 5], supported by advances in con-vex/nonconvex decomposition [6], consensus methods [7], and asynchronous protocols [8]. By distributing control and coordination across many agents (e.g., local controllers, microgrids), DERMS can adapt faster to local demand condi-tions, optimize resource use with lower latency, and maintain the supply-demand balance more effectively. Decentralized approaches also reduce coor-dination costs, enable privacy-preserving transactions [9], and allow parallel computation and data processing [10, 11] without relying on a central infras-tructure. These attributes make DERMS particularly relevant for modern power systems, edge computing applications, and networked resource envi-ronments in smart cities [12]. 

1.1. Related Work 

For broad overviews of distributed optimization and its control appli-cations, we refer to [13, 14]. These surveys are distinguished below from application papers and individual algorithms. The literature on distributed optimization for DERMS has evolved from early linear and ideal multi-agent models [15, 16, 17, 18, 19] to more advanced 2formulations that account for practical non-idealities. 

DERMS optimization architectures. Game-theoretic approaches for mi-crogrid energy management via graph analysis are studied in [20]. Decentral-ized blockchain strategies for distributed energy resources in virtual power plants are developed in [21]. Machine learning and big-data methods for integrating renewable resources into the grid are investigated in [22], with extensions to hybrid energy sources and battery storage in [23]. 

Distributed constrained optimization for economic dispatch. A focused line of research formulates DERMS as distributed constrained optimization, as surveyed in [3]. Foundational gradient-based methods with anytime feasi-bility are proposed in [24, 25, 26]. Extensions include adaptive optimization over unbalanced networks [27], ADMM-based solutions [28, 29, 30, 31], satu-rated dynamics for power ramp-rate limits [32], multi-step gradient descent for economic dispatch [33], and consensus-based multi-agent power flow anal-ysis [34]. 

Accelerated distributed optimization. The convergence rate of distributed algorithms is a critical concern, as faster convergence translates to fewer communication rounds, lower computation overhead, and reduced risk of oscillations when system conditions change. Acceleration via heavy-ball mo-mentum has been studied in the general distributed optimization literature [35, 36], along with Nesterov-type momentum [37] and non-Lipschitz sign-based dynamics for fixed-time convergence [38, 39]. Improved convergence for DERMS specifically is also investigated in [40]. Push-sum and push-pull methods [41, 42] address directed and time-varying topologies but typically do not consider quantization or coupling constraints jointly. 

Communication impairments. Data quantization due to limited chan-nel bit-rate is a significant practical concern. Uniform quantization for dis-tributed optimization is studied in [43, 44, 45], and logarithmic quantization in [46, 47, 48]. Time-delays in distributed algorithms are addressed in several works (e.g., [24]), but typically not jointly with quantization and momentum acceleration. Two recent delay-robust power-system results clarify the scope of the present contribution. Shi et al. [49] treat economic dispatch with hetero-geneous delays, bounded generation, adaptive penalties, and a continuous-time primal-dual/scattering construction. Li et al. [50] establish delay-independent stability for distributed secondary frequency control through a reformulated continuous-time primal-dual controller. In contrast, the present method is a discrete-time signed-gradient allocation scheme with exact preser-3vation of the aggregate equality under paired symmetric delays, fixed log-arithmic quantization, and heavy-ball momentum. Its delay guarantee is delay-dependent and assumes a static undirected graph; it neither subsumes the scattering-based heterogeneous-delay result nor provides frequency-dynamics guarantees. 

1.2. Gap and Contributions 

The main technical contribution is a distributed momentum-based signed gradient-consensus method that preserves the coupling equality at every iter-ation. In particular, paired delays and fixed logarithmic quantization coexist with exact per-iteration conservation of the coupling equality. The delayed fixed-quantized recursion is naturally a hybrid dynamical object: continuous resource states evolve through discrete quantizer-cell and delay selections. The practical-stability theorem is stated for a static connected graph; the feasibility identity itself also holds for paired time-varying undirected edges. The specific results are as follows: (i) Ergodic practical-stability under quantization, momentum, and delays: 

We prove a self-contained Lyapunov-Krasovskii result. The delay mis-match is decomposed into a smooth-gradient history term and an ad-ditive discontinuous-quantization term, and a computable invariant-sublevel condition removes the former a priori orbit assumption. This additive-disturbance treatment avoids any Lipschitz assumption on the discontinuous quantizer and is reusable in delayed quantized-network analyses. The resulting average residual is explicit and scales as O(ρ2)(Theorem 1). The exact-convergence result is recovered in the unquan-tized case ρ = 0 (Corollary 1). (ii) Anytime feasibility under symmetric delays: We prove that the supply-demand coupling constraint is satisfied at every iteration, even in the presence of log-scale quantization, momentum, and bounded symmetric delays (Lemma 9). (iii) Quantized equilibria and approximate optimality: We characterize the equilibria of the fixed-quantized dynamics as quantized-consensus points and show that every such equilibrium is feasible and satisfies an ex-plicit O(ρ2) optimality-gap bound with respect to the penalized prob-lem (Lemma 2 and Proposition 4). The bound is stated using the 4exact logarithmic-quantizer constant and, therefore, remains valid for the quantizer defined. (iv) Spectral momentum characterization: For the delay-free unquantized quadratic case, we derive the exact modal stability condition, the op-timal heavy-ball pair ( ηHB , μ HB ), and its worst-case contraction factor (Proposition 2).

4. Analysis of Feasibility, Optimality, and Convergence 

The analysis proceeds in three layers. First, we study the distributed dy-namics for the smooth penalized equality-constrained problem and establish anytime feasibility together with practical stability of the fixed-quantized algorithm. Second, we characterize the equilibria of the fixed-quantized dy-namics and bound their suboptimality relative to the penalized optimizer. Third, in the special case ρ = 0, the residual vanishes and exact convergence to the penalized optimizer is recovered. The approximation of the origi-nal box-constrained problem by the penalized problem is handled separately through Proposition 1.

Remark 13 (Interpretation of Theorem 1) . Theorem 1 is an ergodic practical-stability result for the fixed-ρ dynamics. 5.9. Discussion and Limitations 

Only Section 5.6 is claimed as a direct numerical certificate for Theorem 1; it reports all constants, satisfies the invariant-sublevel test, and compares cer-tified μ = 0 .09 and μ = 0 cases. The other figures are empirical sensitivity, comparison, or stress studies. They illustrate equality conservation, tran-sient acceleration, the signed generator-battery mechanism, and the effect of quantization, but their high momentum values, switching graphs, or nons-mooth penalties place them outside one or more sufficient assumptions. The complete graph and very small ρ = 10 −4 were chosen to keep the certifi-cate transparent and tractable; this example does not demonstrate a sparse certified network or coarse quantization. Certification on sparse graphs is more conservative because AF grows as λ2 decreases. The certificate itself uses generator nodes only, so B = I, but Section 5.3 now supplies a separate fixed sparse experiment with 12 generators and 8 batteries that directly exer-cises B = diag(+1 , −1) and verifies signed equality conservation numerically. 6. Concluding Remarks and Future Directions 

This paper establishes exact per-iteration conservation of the coupling equality for a distributed signed-gradient method operating jointly with paired delays, fixed logarithmic quantization, and heavy-ball momentum. The asso-ciated discrete-time quantizer-plus-delay recursion is a hybrid system whose continuous states are driven by discrete quantizer-cell and delay selections. 35 Figure 11: Cost residual for nonquadratic smooth strongly convex costs with μ = 0 (dashed) and μ = 0 .5 (solid). In this empirical run, momentum improves the transient and both curves approach the same residual floor. 

Its self-contained Lyapunov-Krasovskii practical-stability theorem applies on a static connected graph under bounded symmetric delays. The proof explicitly separates smooth delayed-gradient variation from discontinuous quantization error, treating the latter as an additive disturbance inside the Krasovskii functional, and uses an invariant-sublevel certificate rather than an assumed compact orbit. Equality feasibility is preserved at every iter-ation; finite smooth penalties, however, do not guarantee per-iterate box feasibility. Quantized equilibria within the certified sublevel have an explicit 

O(ρ2) gap relative to the smooth penalized optimizer, and exact conver-gence to that optimizer is recovered for ρ = 0. Proposition 2 gives an exact momentum-acceleration characterization for the quadratic baseline; the fixed sparse-graph experiment confirms its modal recurrence and asymptotic rate, and the mixed generator-battery experiment directly exercises the signed dynamics. These conservation and discontinuous-disturbance arguments are the main reusable contributions beyond the particular DERMS instance. Several directions remain for future work: 

Optimal momentum selection. The admissible ( ητ , μ ) region defined by conditions (47)-(49) is sufficient but likely conservative. The quadratic delay-free optimum is given by (21); identifying μ∗(ητ , ¯τ , ρ ) for nonlinear delayed quantized dynamics remains open. 

Asymmetric delays. Anytime feasibility is proved under symmetric delays (τij = τji ). Extending to fully asymmetric delays via pairwise exchange buffers or conservation mechanisms is an important open problem. 36 Uniform connectivity. The practical-stability proof currently requires a static connected graph. A windowed Lyapunov argument and a delay model compatible with changing edges would be needed for B-connectivity. 

Scalability and nonlinear costs. Systematic experiments on large-scale networks and nonlinear cost models would further validate practical applica-bility. 

Directed graphs. The feasibility proof relies on undirected communication. Extending via push-sum or push-pull mechanisms [41, 42] while preserving anytime feasibility remains open.
