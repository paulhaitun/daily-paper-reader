# Task Offloading and Resource Allocation for Deterministic Service Provisioning in the IoT–Edge– Cloud Continuum 

K. Aghababaiyan *, B. Coll-Perales *, and J. Gozalvez *

> *

Networked Systems Laboratory, Universidad Miguel Hernández de Elche, 03202 Elche, Spain 

Abstract —Future networks will seamlessly integrate computing and communication resources within a unified ecosystem of IoT devices, edge nodes, and cloud servers, forming an IoT-edge-cloud continuum. Within such  an  ecosystem, tasks can be dynamically offloaded to leverage resources across the continuum and efficiently scale with demand. Task offloading has traditionally focused on minimizing task execution latency or energy consumption. In contrast, we propose adeterministic task offloading and resource allocation scheme for the joint management of communication and computing resources to support services with deterministic service-level requirements. The proposed scheme aims to maximize the number of tasks completed on time, rather than minimizing the latency of individual tasks. To do so, it exploits variable task latency requirements to distribute the workload across the continuum. It can also prioritize high-priority tasks under resource scarcity. Numerical evaluations demonstrate that a deterministic task offloading strategy scales more effectively and can better guarantee timely task completion by balancing resource utilization across the continuum. 

Keywords —Continuum, edge-cloud, deterministic, task offloading, resource allocation, IoT. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

The Internet of Things (IoT) is driving transformative changes across various domains, including connected and automated mobility and smart manufacturing.

Critical services require efficient data processing for timely decision-making, which can place significant processing demands on computing resources as the complexity and number of tasks grow and requires careful latency management to meet deadlines of time-sensitive operations. In this context, the IoT-edge-cloud continuum has emerged as a key enabler to address increasing computational and latency challenges. However, in critical verticals such as connected and automated mobility and smart manufacturing, it is usually more important to satisfy stringent deterministic service constraints - particularly in terms of bounded deadline compliance  [4]  than minimizing latency. Connected vehicles typically feature considerable onboard processing capabilities but operate in highly dynamic environments with fluctuating connectivity conditions and diverse service requirements. In addition, software-defined vehicles need to support functionalities updated and expanded over time with the same computing resources. Smart factories usually operate within (semi-) static and controlled network infrastructures but deploy a large variety of devices and require collaborative efforts between devices for manufacturing task execution. In this context, achieving the appropriate balance between local processing and task offloading is essential. While offloading to edge or cloud servers can expand available computing capacity, it may introduce additional latency. On the other hand, relying solely on local execution may be limited by the local processing capabilities. The key challenge lies in designing task offloading strategies that holistically manage resources across the continuum in a manner that prevents communication and computing bottlenecks, preserves balanced resource utilization, and guarantees deterministic service levels  [5] .

Most existing latency-based task offloading schemes 

[6] -[9]  focus on minimizing individual task latency, which risks overloading resources if too many tasks are assigned to the nodes that individually can minimize latency, causing the system to operate in a locally optimal but globally inefficient manner. This paper advances the state of the art with a deterministic task offloading and resource allocation strategy designed to satisfy deterministic service-level requirements while effectively scaling by balancing workloads across the continuum. The proposed scheme focuses on completing tasks within bounded deadlines rather than minimizing individual task latency. By exploiting the available task latency budget, it balances workload distribution across the IoT –edge –cloud continuum, which helps prevent computing and communication congestions in specific computing units and communication links. The scheme has the ability to prioritize the timely execution of critical tasks under resource scarcity. Its potential and adaptability to heterogeneous IoT operational settings are demonstrated in representative connected mobility and smart manufacturing scenarios characterized by different processing capabilities and diverse task requirements. Numerical evaluations show that the proposed deterministic strategy can scale better and successfully complete a higher number of tasks on time than state-of-the-art schemes focused on minimizing individual task latency thanks to a more balanced workload distribution across the continuum.

II. STATE OF THE ART 

A key aspect in managing the IoT-edge-cloud continuum is determining the optimal distribution of task processing across resources in the continuum. This should be done noting that not all IoT nodes are resource-constrained; for instance, devices such as Connected and Automated Vehicles (CAVs) often feature powerful local processors.  In  recent years, growing attention has been devoted to developing task offloading and resource allocation schemes across different computing nodes of the continuum - ranging from local-edge cooperation to cloud-based and fully integrated IoT-edge-cloud continuum - with the goal of minimizing latency and improving the efficiency of distributed computing. The work in  [10]  focuses on latency-aware task execution in mobile edge computing, where computation occurs locally or at nearby edge servers. It optimizes resource allocation and offloading decisions to reduce latency, though it remains confined to the edge and local IoT nodes. Studies  [6]  and  [7]  expand this model by employing the computing capacity of interconnected IoT nodes, specifically vehicles, as distributed resources. In  [6] ,parked and moving vehicles cooperate with an edge server to process offloaded tasks and minimize total priority-weighted processing latency through coordinated offloading among vehicles, and between vehicles and the edge. The study reported in  [7] 

introduces greater flexibility by enabling vehicles to decide whether to execute tasks locally, offload them to a roadside unit, or share them directly with other vehicles. These studies emphasize the role of vehicles as mobile IoT devices that augment edge resources, contributing to latency reduction.  [8]  introduces an inverse offloading framework, where tasks are transferred from the edge back to vehicles. This scheme alleviates the computational burden on the edge server while maintaining low latency by effectively exploiting underused vehicular computing capacity.  [11]  focuses on cloud-centered task migration, and designs 3Submitted to Future Generation Computer Systems Journal (Elsevier)

The study examines how centralized execution and 5G connectivity can meet increasing computational demands while maintaining latency below the required deadline constraints.  [9]  and  [12]

Studies  [13] -[15]  focus on load balancing and spatial load distribution across resources in the continuum. Collectively, these studies highlight the potential of the IoT –edge –cloud continuum, and the importance of task offloading schemes to effectively exploit the continuum. To date, contributions have mostly focused on minimizing individual task execution latency. However, this focus increases the risk of overloading certain computing units and communication resources, while many vertical services require deterministic service levels and not necessarily minimizing the latency of each task. This paper addresses this gap with a deterministic task offloading and resource allocation strategy that prioritizes the timely completion of tasks within predefined deadlines and a balanced distribution of workloads across the continuum for better scaling. An initial version of this work was presented in  [16] .This manuscript provides a robust extension to that past study by introducing a load distribution mechanism and a priority-based version of the proposed scheme. In addition, we conduct a thorough analysis on the impact of distinct operational environments —such as connected vehicular mobility and smart industrial manufacturing scenarios —demonstrating the performance, robustness, and adaptation of the proposed strategy under diverse network and device characteristics.

We consider two representative IoT-edge-cloud scenarios from the connected mobility and smart manufacturing verticals to evaluate the ability of the task offloading schemes to adapt to heterogeneous operational environments. These scenarios exhibit fundamentally different characteristics in terms of device heterogeneity, local processing capabilities, and reliance on network-assisted computation. The connected mobility scenario includes 𝐽 𝑣 1 =4 smartwatches, 𝐽 𝑣 2 =4 smartphones, and 𝐽 𝑣 3 =2 vehicles, resulting in a total of 𝐽 𝑣 =10 IoT devices. Tasks are generated by these devices at proportions of 𝛼 𝑣 1 =0.1, 

𝛼 𝑣 2 =0.5 and 𝛼 𝑣 3 =0.4, respectively. These values align with the standard distribution of tasks commonly observed across these specific device categories  [19] .The computational workload of each task is modeled as a uniformly distributed random variable whose range depends on the originating device type. Tasks generated by smart vehicles are more computationally demanding, ranging from 75 to 150 Mcycles. In contrast, tasks generated by smartwatches and smartphones range from 25 to 35 Mcycles and from 30 to 60 Mcycles, respectively, reflecting the less demanding applications they support  [20] . Following the specifications of commercial off-the-shelf products, the local processing capabilities are set to 400 MHz for smartwatches, 3 GHz for smartphones, and 12 GHz for vehicles, capturing the large disparity in onboard computing power across device types. 

In the smart manufacturing scenario, we consider a medium-size industrial deployment  [21]  with 𝐽 𝐼 1 = 5 mobile IoT devices, 𝐽 𝐼 2 = 6 assembly robots, 𝐽 𝐼 3 = 6 advanced factory robots, and 𝐽 𝐼4 = 6 automated guided vehicles, for a total of 𝐽 𝐼 = 23 IoT devices. VII. CONCLUSIONS 

This study presents a Deterministic task offloading and resource allocation scheme for the joint management of communication and computing resources in the IoT –edge –cloud continuum. The proposed scheme prioritizes meeting task deadlines rather than minimizing individual task latency. By leveraging the heterogeneity of task latency requirements, it enables efficient workload distribution and supports a higher number of tasks within their deadlines. In contrast, schemes that aim to minimize the latency of each individual task may fail to efficiently exploit the resources of the IoT –edge –cloud continuum or may impose excessive loads on specific resources. The conducted evaluation demonstrates that a Deterministic scheme of task offloading, and resource allocation better adapts to variations in operating conditions, as well as to changes in resource availability and characteristics across the continuum. By dynamically adjusting the balance between local processing and offloading according to scenario-specific capabilities, the Deterministic scheme efficiently utilizes available resources across the continuum in diverse operational settings and can guarantee deterministic service levels for high-priority tasks, even under resource-constrained scenarios.
