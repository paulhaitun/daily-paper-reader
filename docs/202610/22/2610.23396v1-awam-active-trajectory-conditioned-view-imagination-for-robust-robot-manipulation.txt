AWAM: ACTIVE TRAJECTORY-CONDITIONED VIEW
IMAGINATION FOR ROBUST ROBOT MANIPULATION
Jiawei Geng1, Zhenyang Liu1,2, Yuzheng Wu1, Yanwei Fu1,2
1Fudan University
2Shanghai Innovation Institute
ABSTRACT
Rapid progress in embodied intelligence has enabled increasingly capable robots
to follow language instructions and manipulate diverse objects. However, strong
results in structured environments do not automatically transfer to open-world
conditions, where clutter, occlusion, and changing viewpoints can obscure the
spatial relationships required for action. To address this perceptual gap, a vir-
tual active-perception paradigm is introduced. AWAM, an Active World-Action
Model actively plans where to observe and imagines what will be seen along the
planned trajectory before acting. Task-related virtual camera motion is predicted
and used as a structured query into a geometry-conditioned world model, produc-
ing a reusable spatial prior for manipulation, which is later compressed into a fixed
memory and fused with live observations through cross-attention for diffusion-
based action prediction. AWAM achieves competitive success rates with 75.9%
on LIBERO-Occ, 62.4% on LIBERO-Plus, 76.5% on RLBench-OG Occlusion1,
and 94.4% in real-robot trials. These results support improved robustness to the
evaluated occlusions and distribution shifts, addressing part of the gap between
structured manipulation and less controlled environments.

INTRODUCTION
Embodied intelligence has advanced rapidly through large-scale robot learning, vision–language
pretraining, and generative action modeling. Increasingly capable policies can translate language
goals and visual observations into manipulation behaviors across diverse tasks (Brohan et al., 2022;
2023; Ghosh et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2024; Black et al., 2025; Chi et al., 2025).

However, much of this progress is evaluated in relatively structured settings in which camera config-
urations, scene layouts, and task-relevant objects remain sufficiently observable. Occlusion-focused
and distribution-shift benchmarks expose a complementary limitation: once deployment becomes
less structured, an unfavorable camera pose, clutter, or a shifted layout can hide the target or interac-
tion region and remove the evidence required for control (Li et al., 2026; Fei et al., 2026; Bai et al.,
2026; Liu et al., 2025c;d). This creates a perceptual bottleneck rather than only an action-modeling
bottleneck. More expressive action models alone cannot directly recover scene evidence that is ab-
sent or highly ambiguous in the input, so performance gains obtained under well-observed scenes
may not transfer to open-world conditions with incomplete visibility.
Several lines of work have begun to address this problem. Physical active perception acquires ad-
ditional evidence by moving the camera or robot, but introduces extra execution steps and can alter
the robot or scene state. Virtual-view methods avoid this cost by selecting or synthesizing com-
plementary observations. Candidate-view approaches choose from predefined cameras (Liu et al.,
2026b; Xu et al., 2026); TVVE predicts task-conditioned virtual poses and re-renders reconstructed
geometry (Bai et al., 2026); and world-action models such as Motus and Fast-WAM exploit predic-
tive visual modeling to strengthen action representations (Bi et al., 2026; Yuan et al., 2026). These
directions establish the value of additional visual context, yet the underlying limitation is not fully
resolved: virtual perception is often restricted to isolated views, reconstruction-based rendering
cannot reveal surfaces absent from the initial geometry, and predictive world models are commonly
organized around action-induced futures rather than deliberate viewpoint queries.
To address these questions, task-conditioned virtual active perception is formulated as trajectory-
conditioned view imagination. Instead of selecting a single view or a small set of independent
poses, a virtual camera trajectory specifies how perception should evolve to expose task-relevant ev-
idence. Such a trajectory can reorient toward a target, approach fine interaction geometry, or move
around an occluder when no single viewpoint is sufficient. Importantly, the predicted trajectory is
never physically executed; it serves only as a sequence of virtual queries, thus retrieving the avail-
able evidence and learned spatial priors into a representation that better supports control, separating
perceptual exploration from robot motion.
Based on this formulation, AWAM is introduced as an Active World-Action Model that realizes the
virtual-perception process before closed-loop control. A task-conditioned trajectory predictor first
produces virtual camera poses for better observation from the initial RGB-D image and language
instruction. The calibrated observations are then back-projected into coarse 3D geometry and ren-
dered along the predicted path. A geometry-conditioned world model fills unsupported regions and
produces multi-view latent features, which are compressed into a persistent world memory. During
manipulation, live static- and wrist-camera observations retrieve relevant predictive context from
this memory, and a diffusion policy generates short-horizon action chunks in closed loop. The re-
sulting design uses world modeling as an internal perceptual mechanism for asking where to look
and what would be seen, rather than as a prediction of the robot’s executed future.
Evaluation is conducted across three simulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation. Success
reaches 75.9% on LIBERO-Occ, 62.4% on LIBERO-Plus, 76.5 ± 1.1% on RLBench-OG Occlu-
sion1, and 94.4% in real-robot trials. On LIBERO-Occ, removing the entire predictive-memory
branch reduces average success by 11.5 percentage points, and replacing learned task-conditioned
virtual trajectory with sampled viewpoints reduces it by 5.7 points. Together, these results demon-
strate the effectiveness of learned virtual camera motion and persistent predictive memory for ma-
nipulation under incomplete visibility. The contributions are threefold:
• A trajectory-conditioned virtual active-perception paradigm is introduced for manipulation,
replacing isolated or physically executed viewpoint acquisition with a continuous sequence
of task-directed virtual queries.
• AWAM is proposed to transform the predicted observation trajectory into geometry-
consistent multi-view representations and compress them into persistent world memory
as a reusable spatial prior for manipulation under partial observability.
• Extensive evaluations across three challenging simulation benchmarks and real-world ma-
nipulation demonstrate strong performance under occlusion and distribution shifts and con-
firm the benefits of learned camera motion and predictive memory through ablations.

RELATED WORK
Robot learning and action generation.
Large-scale robot policies map visual observations and
language instructions directly to actions. Recent VLA models and diffusion-based policies improve
generalization, action representation, and efficient decoding through large-scale pretraining and ex-
pressive generative objectives (Brohan et al., 2022; 2023; Ghosh et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2024;
Black et al., 2025; Chi et al., 2025; Pertsch et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025a;b). These
methods primarily focus on how actions are represented and learned from available observations.
In contrast, AWAM targets the perceptual evidence supplied to the policy, constructing additional
task-conditioned spatial context when relevant scene information is poorly observed or occluded.
Multiview manipulation under occlusion.
Occlusion can be mitigated by acquiring or synthesiz-
ing complementary visual evidence through multiview perception, viewpoint selection, and imag-
ined views (Liu et al., 2026b; Bai et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026; Xu et al., 2026). Existing approaches
typically rely on additional sensing, reconstructed views, or a small fixed number of generated obser-
vations. AWAM instead predicts a task-conditioned virtual camera trajectory and consolidates the
generated multi-view sequence into persistent world memory for closed-loop manipulation. This
formulation turns occlusion handling into trajectory-conditioned predictive perception, where the
model jointly reasons about where to observe, what would be observed, and how this information
should support subsequent control.
World models for robotic manipulation.
Recent video diffusion and world models have shown
strong capability in modeling long-horizon visual dynamics, while text-, action-, and camera-
conditioned variants further enable controllable prediction of future or novel observations (Zhang
et al., 2025a; 2026; Liu et al., 2026a). Predictive world models provide representations of how visual
observations may evolve and have increasingly been integrated with robot control. Video Predic-
tion Policy (Hu et al., 2025) conditions control on features learned from predictive video modeling.
Motus (Bi et al., 2026) unifies visual understanding, video generation, inverse dynamics, and action
prediction within a latent world-action model. Fast-WAM (Yuan et al., 2026) separates the benefits
of video co-training from explicit test-time future generation and shows that strong action predic-
tion can be retained without iterative future-video rollout. Unlike approaches centered on predicting
the future scene induced by robot actions, AWAM uses world modeling as a form of active per-
ception. A task-conditioned camera trajectory specifies where the current scene should be queried,
and a geometry-conditioned world model predicts visual representations along this trajectory. These
representations are consolidated into a compact world memory and reused throughout manipulation
rather than interpreted as the robot’s executed future.

3.2
TASK-CONDITIONED VIRTUAL VIEW PLANNING
An unfavorable initial wrist-camera pose can hide the object needed for manipulation, leaving the
policy to act on incomplete visual evidence. Rather than relying on the initial viewpoint alone,
a task-conditioned camera trajectory is predicted to specify where additional virtual observations
should be queried for better scene perception.

trajectory is parameterized relative to its initial wrist-camera pose, making prediction invariant to

Rendering P0 at the predicted camera poses preserves evidence from observed surfaces, but leaves
holes where the reconstruction has no support. Such renderings become increasingly incomplete
under viewpoint changes, since surfaces that are occluded or outside the initial fields of view have
no geometric support, often resulting in large holes and unreliable visual features.
WorldStereo (Zhang et al., 2026) is employed as a geometry-conditioned world model and adapted
to manipulation scenes. Intermediate geometry-conditioned features are extracted instead of passing
generated RGB video to the action model, following the predictive-representation perspective of Hu
et al. (2025). The generation target is an alternate view of the initial scene: objects and the robot are
intended to remain fixed while the virtual camera changes pose.
The memory encoder compresses the predicted sequence. It aggregates spatial features within each
predicted view and subsequently exchanges information across virtual viewpoints through inter-
view attention. The resulting fixed-size token set summarizes the trajectory-level visual prediction
while remaining efficient to retrieve during control, capturing what the robot is expected to observe
from task-relevant viewpoints while remaining grounded in the geometry of the current scene.
3.4
MEMORY-CONDITIONED CLOSED LOOP CONTROL
At each control step, the current static- and wrist-camera observations are encoded into live visual
tokens Lt. Predictive context is retrieved from the cached world memory M0 using residual cross-
attention,
Ft = Lt + CA(Lt, M0),
(7)
where Lt serves as the attention query and M0 provides the keys and values. Although the memory
remains fixed throughout the episode, its retrieved content changes with the current observation.
This allows the policy to access different parts of the predictive context as the manipulation state
evolves, while the residual connection preserves a direct pathway from live perception to action
prediction.
A conditional diffusion transformer is used to generate short-horizon action chunks. Given the fused
visual representation Ft, proprioceptive state st, and language instruction ℓ, the policy predicts
bAt = Dθ (zσ, σ; Ft, st, ℓ) ,
(8)
where zσ denotes the noisy action representation at diffusion level σ. The policy is trained on
demonstrated action chunks using a standard diffusion denoising objective. During both action-
policy training and evaluation, M0 is constructed from the trajectory predicted by Gϕ rather than
from the demonstrated wrist-camera trajectory, avoiding a train–test mismatch in the memory input.
During closed-loop execution, the geometry-conditioned memory is constructed once from the ini-
tial observation and retained throughout the episode. At each replanning step, the policy receives
fresh visual and proprioceptive observations, retrieves relevant predictive context, and generates a
new action chunk from the updated state. The cached memory therefore acts as persistent predictive
context about task-relevant virtual viewpoints rather than as a continually updated scene representa-
tion. Live observations remain the primary source of information about the evolving manipulation
state, while the memory provides complementary spatial evidence that may be unavailable from the
current viewpoint.

4.2
EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
Results on LIBERO-OCC.
As shown in Table 1, AWAM achieves an average success rate of
75.9%. The improvement is consistent across three of the four suites: 84.6% on Spatial, 93.2% on
Object, and 84.8% on Goal. They benefit from obtaining task-relevant evidence that is difficult to in-
fer from the current view: the predicted viewpoint sequence queries the initially hidden region, while
the world model converts those queries into a persistent representation that can be reused during ma-
nipulation. On Long, however, Fast-WAM obtains the highest success rate (44.2%), while AWAM
reaches 41.0%. Long-horizon tasks remain challenging because the cached memory is constructed
from the initial scene and is not regenerated after substantial object-state changes, which might be
solved by regenerating memory each time when gripper state changes. Nevertheless, AWAM attains
the highest overall average, indicating that active observations remain useful across a broad range of
occluded manipulation problems.
Results on RLBench-OG.
Table 2 presents the quantitative results on the Occlusion1 split of
RLBench-OG. AWAM achieves an average success rate of 76.5%. The improvement is evident
on tasks where the manipulation target or an interaction-relevant region is difficult to infer from
the nominal observation. For example, AWAM reaches 94.7% success on water plants, improving
over TVVE (88.0%), ARP (66.0%), and RVT2 (72.0%), and obtains 92.0% on scoop with spatula.
It further achieves 100% success on five other tasks. Performance nevertheless remains low on
block pyramid and solve puzzle. Both require precise multi-stage interaction, for which recovering
visibility alone is insufficient. This suggests that predictive observation is most useful when partial
observability is a primary bottleneck, but does not eliminate the intrinsic control difficulty of high-
precision long-horizon tasks. Nevertheless, the overall result and the favorable average rank in
Table 2 show that the benefit of active world memory extends across a diverse set of occlusion-
sensitive tasks and transfers beyond the LIBERO environment.

4.3
ABLATION STUDY AND QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS
Component Analysis.
To disentangle the contributions of each component, four controlled vari-
ants are evaluated while keeping the policy backbone, training data, and evaluation protocol un-
changed. Results are summarized in Table 5.
Variant(a) shows that removing the entire predictive branch and relying only on live observations
reduces the success rate from 75.9% to 64.4%, corresponding to the largest drop of 11.5 percentage
points. This result confirms that the performance gain cannot be explained by the closed-loop pol-
icy alone, and that information constructed before control provides important complementary visual
context when the current observations are incomplete or occluded. Variant(b), where the predicted
camera trajectory is directly provided to the live policy without virtual-view generation or memory
construction, performance improves to 67.3%. The 2.9-point gain over the live-only variant suggests
that the task-conditioned trajectory itself carries a useful implicit manipulation prior. However, its
substantial gap to the full model indicates that trajectory conditioning alone cannot substitute for
explicit predictive visual context. Variant(c), which replaces the learned trajectory with spherical
viewpoints uniformly sampled around the whole scene region achieves 70.2%. Despite preserving
view diversity as well as the same generation and memory modules, the 5.7-point degradation shows
that generic coverage of the scene is less effective than allocating virtual observations according to a
learned, task-conditioned trajectory. Variant(d) retaining the learned trajectory while removing gen-
erative prediction and constructing memory only from warped geometric observations yields 69.0%.
This variant preserves the views queried by the model, but can only reproject evidence already vis-
ible in the initial RGB-D observations. Its 6.9-point drop indicates that viewpoint selection alone
is insufficient under severe occlusion, where previously unobserved surfaces cannot be recovered
through geometric warping.
Taken together, these ablations reveal complementary roles of the proposed components. The pre-
dicted trajectory provides both task-dependent viewpoint selection and a weak planning prior, while
generative prediction converts these queries into predictive visual context beyond the initially ob-
served geometry. Compressing this predicted evidence into memory further allows the closed-loop
8


policy to access such information throughout manipulation. The full combination therefore achieves
the highest success rate of 75.9% on LIBERO-Occ.
Sensitivity to Virtual-View Budget.
The trajectory length K determines the amount of virtual
visual context constructed before control. K varies in {7, 14, 21, 28} while keeping all other set-
tings fixed. Performance improves as the trajectory provides more virtual observations and saturates
around K = 21, with success rates of 72.4%, 74.8%, 75.9% and 76.0% on LIBERO-Occ respec-
tively. Increasing the length further to 28 brings no meaningful improvement, suggesting limited
sensitivity to the exact choice of K once sufficient task-relevant scene coverage is obtained.

5
CONCLUSION
Rather than relying only on the currently visible camera frames, AWAM predicts a task-conditioned
virtual camera trajectory and uses a geometry-conditioned world model to imagine observations
along this trajectory. The resulting representations are compressed into a persistent world memory
and jointly used with live observations during closed-loop action prediction. Experiments across
occlusion-focused and distribution-shift benchmarks show that this additional predictive context
improves manipulation robustness when task-relevant objects or interaction regions are poorly ob-
served. These results suggest that world models can serve not only as predictors of future dynamics,
but also as internal perceptual mechanisms for querying spatial information that is missing from the
current observation. Future work could selectively refresh memory when large state changes are
detected, extending virtual active perception to longer-horizon and more dynamic manipulation.

Memory compression.
The predictive features from the virtual trajectory contain dense spatial
information across multiple viewpoints. Rather than exposing all features directly to the action
policy, they are compressed with a lightweight query-based memory encoder.
For each virtual view, a small set of learned queries first aggregates its spatial features through
cross-attention. Queries occupying the same slot then exchange information across the virtual-view
sequence through inter-view attention.

duration of the episode. This representation preserves trajectory-level predictive context. Detailed
architectural dimensions of the reference implementation are reported in Table 6.
A.1.3
MEMORY-CONDITIONED CLOSED-LOOP POLICY
Live visual encoding.
At each replanning step, the latest static- and wrist-camera RGB observa-
tions are encoded by a shared visual backbone. Camera-specific embeddings preserve the identity
of the two visual streams, after which a language-conditioned resampler converts the spatial features
into a fixed-size set of live visual tokens Lt.
Unlike the predictive memory, which is constructed once from the initial observation, Lt is recom-
puted from fresh observations during execution. It therefore provides the policy with up-to-date
information about the evolving manipulation state.
Memory retrieval.
Predictive information is retrieved from the cached memory using residual
cross-attention, where the live representation provides the attention queries and the cached memory
provides keys and values.
Although M0 remains fixed during an episode, the attention weights depend on the current obser-
vation. Consequently, different predictive information can be retrieved as the manipulation state
evolves. The residual pathway also preserves direct access to the current visual evidence.

A.3
TRAINING PROCEDURE
The modules are optimized sequentially rather than through end-to-end backpropagation. The tra-
jectory predictor is trained first, followed by world-model adaptation and then the action-policy
stages.
Trajectory model.
The trajectory model is initialized from a pretrained text–RGB-D camera tra-
jectory generator and adapted using demonstrated wrist-camera paths. The visual and language
backbone encoders remain frozen, while the trajectory-specific projection and autoregressive pre-
diction layers are optimized with the teacher-forced objective described in the main paper.
World model.
The geometry-conditioned world model (Zhang et al., 2026) is adapted to manipu-
lation scenes while retaining its pretrained geometric and camera-conditioning capabilities. Training
targets correspond to alternative observations of the same initial scene under different camera poses.
Thus, the model learns to predict viewpoint-conditioned scene representations rather than robot-
induced future states.
During action-policy training, world-model features are precomputed and cached. This avoids re-
peatedly running the large predictive model during policy optimization and provides a fixed repre-
sentation interface between predictive perception and action learning.
Action policy.
Training is divided into an interface-alignment stage and an action-model adapta-
tion stage. The first stage optimizes the live visual projection, resampler, memory encoder, and
memory-fusion modules while keeping the pretrained action denoiser fixed.
The second stage
freezes these connection modules and adapts the diffusion action model.

A.5
LIMITATIONS
The current system relies on calibrated cameras and depth information to construct geometry-aware
virtual observations, which introduces sensing assumptions beyond purely image-based policies.
Besides, the current system constructs M0 once from the initial scene and keeps it fixed throughout
manipulation. When objects are substantially displaced or articulated, parts of the cached memory
may become stale. Fresh live observations provide the primary control feedback, but the mem-
ory itself is not explicitly updated. Dynamically scheduled memory reconstruction is a promising
direction for long-horizon tasks.
