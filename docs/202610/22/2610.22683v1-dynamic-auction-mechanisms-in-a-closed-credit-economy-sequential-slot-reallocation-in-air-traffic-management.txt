# Dynamic Auction Mechanisms in a Closed-Credit Economy: Sequential Slot Reallocation in Air Traffic Management 

Jihun Kim, Pan-Yang Su, Maria G. Mendoza, Victoria Marie Tuck, Chinmay Maheshwari, S. Shankar Sastry 

Abstract — Indivisible resources allocated in advance may need to be reallocated when capacity reductions make existing assignments infeasible, yet monetary compensation is often impractical or undesirable. We develop a sequential resource reallocation scheme using reusable artificial credits, where agents hold pre-existing resource rights and have heterogeneous marginal valuations over time blocks. Capacity reductions produce fractional endowments, which our closed-credit scheme resolves into indivisible final allocations. We model agents’ intertemporal preferences through a predicted-value-to-go func-tion and characterize conditions ensuring that their optimal demand is non-increasing in price. We propose an ascending-auction mechanism that partitions agents into subgroups and uses the minimum subgroup market-clearing price as a common transaction price. We show that the mechanism preserves non-myopic individual rationality and weak nontrader neutrality. Moreover, we establish Pareto efficiency under optimal-demand clearing in the unified market and bound the expected gain from misreporting by the probability that an agent changes the common price. Simulations of airport slot reallocation demonstrate reductions in total modeled delay and cancellation costs relative to a ground-delay baseline. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

We study dynamic auction markets in which long-lived agents enter with existing resource endowments, resource capacities are uncertain, and monetary transfers are restricted. Such problems arise when scarce resource rights are al-located in advance but must subsequently be adjusted as uncertain capacities are realized. Unlike a sequence of static allocation problems, previous allocations create endowments: agents retain rights to previously allocated resources, so subsequent reallocation requires exchange among agents. This intertemporal exchange structure arises naturally in transportation capacity, advance reservations, and other set-tings in which usage rights are allocated before uncertainty is fully resolved. Our goal is to design a mechanism with desirable eco-nomic properties, including Pareto efficiency, incentive com-patibility, individual rationality, and weak nontrader neutral-ity, a counterpart of weak budget balance in our model. Our mechanism builds on artificial currencies. Although credits have no intrinsic value, they can acquire endogenous value by providing access to resources in future markets. We capture this future benefit through a predicted-value-to-go function that enters agents’ utilities and influences their willingness to

Theoretical difficulties. Even when predicted-value-to-go functions are affine with strictly positive slopes, which yield quasilinear preferences over current allocations and net credit transfers, the mechanism-design problem remains challenging. Unlike bilateral trade and conventional double auctions [3], [4], [5], agents are not exogenously designated as buyers or sellers. Each agent enters with a resource endowment and may become a buyer or a seller depending on its valuation, credit balance, and the market price. Moreover, unlike standard multi-unit auctions with budget constraints [6], the mechanism must accommodate both sides of the market while respecting agents’ credit constraints. Our first result, Theorem 1, demonstrates a fundamental limitation of this exchange problem. We show that Pareto efficiency, incentive compatibility, invidual rationality, and weak nontrader neutrality cannot in general be achieved simultaneously. The result follows by embedding bilat-eral trade as a special case and applying the Myerson– Satterthwaite impossibility theorem [3]. 

Proposed mechanism. Motivated by this impossibility, we propose an ascending-auction mechanism (Algorithm 1) that preserves individual rationality and weak nontrader neutrality. We characterize conditions on the predicted-value-to-go functions that ensure price-monotone demand (Propo-sition 3), which lays the foundation for ascending price discovery. Building on budget-constrained multi-unit auc-tions [6] and multi-unit double auctions [5], the mechanism partitions agents into G subgroups, conducts price discovery within each subgroup, and uses the minimum subgroup price as the common transaction price. Subgrouping restricts trading opportunities but can also limit an agent’s influence on the common price. We establish a conditional Pareto-efficiency guarantee when G = 1 (Theorem 3) and bound manipulation gains in terms of the probability of changing the common price (Theorem 4). All proofs are deferred to the supplementary material. 

Airport slot allocation. We apply our mechanism to airport slot allocation [7]. Airlines plan flights well in ad-vance, while available capacity remains uncertain. Using our mechanism, we develop a day-of market that uses reusable arXiv:2610.22683v1 [cs.GT] 15 Oct 2026 credits to elicit airlines’ demands and compensate them for releasing capacity across successive time blocks. We evaluate its performance through simulations. II. R ELATED WORK 

Dynamic allocation without money. Resource alloca-tion with artificial currencies has a long history, including pseudomarkets [8] and approximate competitive equilibrium from equal incomes [9]. A growing literature extends this idea to repeated resource-allocation problems, including spot mechanisms [10] and dynamic approximate competitive equilibrium from equal incomes [11]. These approaches typically focus on repeated resource allocation problems, while we deal with exchange problems where agents have endowments. Banerjee et al. [12] study reusable resources with unit demand within each round and potentially multi-round usage requirements. Our agents may demand multiple units within a block, with different marginal valuations, and trade relative to pre-existing resource endowments. Karma mechanisms [1], [2] are particularly related to our setting, where they use artificial currencies, called “karma,” to coordinate resource allocation. Their models, however, differ from ours. Elokda et al. [1] study repeated competition for a resource with heterogeneous urgency but without initial resource endowments, while Vuppalapati et al. [2] incorpo-rate fair shares but focus on demand quantities rather than heterogeneous marginal valuations. Our model combines het-erogeneous multi-unit valuations with credit-based exchange of resource rights under capacity uncertainty. A related literature studies dynamic exchange, including [13], [14]. These models, however, are motivated primarily by organ exchange and have a different allocation structure from ours. In particular, organ-exchange models typically focus on one-to-one or compatibility-constrained matching, while our application involves allocating capacity to agents with potentially multi-unit demand. Methodologically, our approach is closely related to [15], which uses artificial currencies to translate monetary mecha-nisms into approximately incentive-compatible nonmonetary mechanisms in repeated allocation environments. Our setting differs because previously allocated rights become endow-ments and reallocation therefore takes the form of exchange. As a result, the guarantees developed for repeated assignment problems do not carry over directly. 

Bilateral Exchange and Double Auctions. The difficulty of exchange under private information was first formalized by Myerson and Satterthwaite [3], in what is now known as the Myerson–Satterthwaite theorem. Subsequent work extends this impossibility to richer exchange environments, including settings with multiple goods or multiple agents [16], [17]. More recently, [18] studies a two-stage market in which previously allocated resources are subsequently exchanged and derives related impossibility results. Our work is closely related to this line of research, but considers exchange mediated by an artificial currency whose value derives from future allocation opportunities. People have proposed methods to get around the impos-sibility results by trading off different properties. Our work is related to double auctions [4]. Recently, Segal-Halevi et al. proposed a truthful multi-unit double-auction mechanism [5]. They split the agents into two groups, run auctions separately, and exchange the prices. This mechanism ensures truthfulness. Our model is more difficult in two aspects. First, their models assume that the roles are exogeneously given, but our agents can be either sellers or buyers depending on the prices. Second, our agents have budget constraints, so naively swapping the prices may result in budget constraint violation. 

Airspace market design. Previous work considers me-diated slot trading [7], preference-based slot scheduling [19], combinatorial exchanges [20], and auction-based slot reallocation [21]. These approaches either rely on real-money transfers, which may be undesirable or impractical in operational airspace markets, or lack an alternative currency to facilitate exchange. Our work bridges this gap by adopting artificial credits as a medium of exchange, enabling flexible slot reallocation without real-money transactions. Credit-based approaches have also been explored in airspace management, albeit for different purposes. In [22], [23], airlines assign credits to flights to influence rerouting and delay decisions, but not theoretical analysis is given. In [24], these credits serve as flight-specific delay-cost weights in a traffic flow optimization model, which is different from our use an artificial currency. More recently, Im et al. [25] study decentralized negotia-tion through asset trading, using regulatory oversight to guide consensus toward efficient and equitable outcomes. Their focus is coordination over a common decision, whereas ours is the sequential reallocation of scarce capacity among agents with pre-existing rights. Thus, applying their negotiation mechanism to our setting is computationally infeasible. III. M ODEL 

We study sequential reallocation of indivisible resources when capacity shocks may leave too few items to fulfill agents’ initial allocations (endowments ). We scale these endowments proportionally to the remaining capacity and design a mechanism that allocates integer quantities while ac-counting for the scaled entitlements through credit transfers. Agents receiving fewer items than their scaled entitlements receive credits, while those receiving more pay credits. Credit balances carry forward for use in subsequent blocks.

B. An Impossibility Result 

Now, we show that there is no mechanism that satisfies all the properties in Section IV-A. Our result is a closed-credit analogue of the Myerson–Satterthwaite impossibility theorem [3]. 

Theorem 1 (Impossibility) . Consider a block consisting of a buyer, a seller, and a nontrader with a positive credit balance. If the buyer’s and seller’s valuations are independently distributed with overlapping supports, no mechanism can simultaneously satisfy NIR, WNN, NPE, and NIC. Proof. It suffices to establish impossibility for the base case where the predicted-value-to-go function Jb+1  

> i

(w) is affine in w. Under affine predicted-value-to-go function, the relationship (6) shows that the setting reduces to a standard bilateral trade environment with quasilinear preferences and normalized valuations. Furthermore, by Lemma 1, WNN im-plies WBB in the presence of the positive-balance nontrader. Thus, any mechanism satisfying NPE, NIC, NIR, and WNN would induce a bilateral trading mechanism satisfying Pareto efficiency, incentive compatibility, individual rationality, and weak budget balance under quasilinear preferences, contra-dicting the Myerson–Satterthwaite theorem [3]. Thus, a closed-credit economy does not in general avoid the classical impossibility of jointly achieving efficiency, incentive compatibility, individual rationality, and budget balance. V. We study credit-based markets for sequential resource real-location under capacity uncertainty and existing endowments. We establish an impossibility result for jointly achieving NIR, WNN, NPE, and NIC, and propose a group-splitting ascending-auction mechanism that preserves NIR and WNN while providing conditional efficiency and approximate in-centive guarantees. Simulations illustrate its application to airport slot allocation.
