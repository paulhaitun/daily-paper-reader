## 摘要
本文提出一种分布式资源分配算法，其中每个智能体仅通信其本地代价梯度的符号，每次迭代每条有向链路仅传输一个比特。该算法基于拉普拉斯-梯度动力学，并在每个迭代点保持等式（需求）约束，从而保证无论终止时间如何均具有随时可行性。首先，我们针对固定步长情形给出完整的基于Lyapunov的收敛分析：迭代点最多经过 O(1/ε^2) 次迭代进入优化器邻域，该邻域半径在决策变量上按 O(τ) 缩放，在代价残差上按 O(τ^2) 缩放，其中 τ 为步长。关键在于，步长上界是一个时不变网络常数（仅取决于图拉普拉斯谱和代价曲率界），因此无需自适应调参。其次，我们刻画了在 B-连通、权重平衡有向图下的不变集，该条件严格弱于大多数先前工作所假设的全时连通性和双随机权重，并表明任何奇异的、保号非线性都能产生相同的不变集与下降结构，而单比特协议是更广泛非线性类中通信最小化的极端实例。第三，我们通过光滑罚松弛处理局部盒约束，并显式量化罚权重如何与步长上界和最优性间隙相互作用。在经济调度（1000台发电机）和CPU调度（500台服务器）上的仿真表明，相对于多比特基线，单比特协议在总通信成本上实现了5–60倍的降低，代价是可调且显式有界的最优性间隙。经验上表明递减步长策略可消除该间隙；其形式化收敛分析被指出为一个开放问题。

## Abstract
This paper proposes a distributed resource allocation algorithm in which each agent communicates only the sign of its local cost gradient, a single bit per directed link per iteration. The algorithm is based on Laplacian-gradient dynamics and preserves the equality (demand) constraint at every iterate, guaranteeing anytime feasibility irrespective of termination time.   First, we provide a complete Lyapunov-based convergence analysis for the fixed-step-size regime: the iterates are shown to enter, in at most O(1/\varepsilon^2) iterations, a neighborhood of the optimizer whose radius scales as O(τ) in the decision variable and O(τ^2) in the cost residual, where $τ$ is the step-size. Crucially, the step-size bound is a time-invariant network constant (depending only on the graph Laplacian spectrum and the cost curvature bounds), so no adaptive tuning is required.   Second, we characterize the invariant set under B-connected, weight-balanced digraphs, strictly weaker than the all-time connectivity and doubly-stochastic weights assumed in most prior work, and show that any odd, sign-preserving nonlinearity yields the same invariant-set and descent structure, with the single-bit protocol being the extreme communication-minimizing instance of a broader nonlinear class.   Third, we address local box constraints via smooth penalty relaxation and quantify explicitly how the penalty weight interacts with the step-size bound and the optimality gap.   Simulations on economic dispatch (1000 generators) and CPU scheduling (500 servers) demonstrate that the single-bit protocol achieves 5-60 times reduction in total communication cost relative to multi-bit baselines, at the price of a tunable and explicitly bounded optimality gap. A diminishing step-size schedule is shown empirically to eliminate this gap; its formal convergence analysis is identified as an open problem.
